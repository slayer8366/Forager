None is touched by the diff. This is "far-from-both stays 40 m plus accuracy" in -1631.
- **Mid-return arrival of a line.** The lines are passed per reading rather than fixed at construction, so a re-route's trail part applies on the next fix without restarting the hold (OffTrackJudgeTest, "an also-on line given mid-stray ends the stray without restarting it"). ### 2. "Only the trail parts" against the owner's words (finding 399-1, medium, for the owner)

The owner's text in -1631 is "You're far from the trail and your track." The coder counts as "your track" the path copied at Return (`pathAtReturn`, the stored points before Return, plus the start). Not counted: the **track parts of the way-home line**. For the first line of a return those are stored points before Return, already in the path, as the coder says. For a **re-routed** line the dotted part `trackBack` is "the walker's track from the most recent stored point back to the point they left the trail at" (WayHome.kt:21-22), which is the stray itself, walked **after** Return, and so **not** in `pathAtReturn`.

What follows on a walk: the walker leaves the trail on the way home and reaches 50 m off it; the way home re-routes at once, dotted back along the stray (report 1.3). The walker turns and follows that dotted line back. Standing on it they are more than 40 m from the trail part and, unless the stray happens to run beside the walk out, more than 40 m from `pathAtReturn`. At the 15 s mark they get "You're far from the trail and your track. The line on the map shows the way back." while on the line. RECORD -1589 names the dotted part "your track", so the owner's sentence can be read to include it.

The coder's reading is the report's 8.4 verbatim ("every track walked before Return and every trail line shown on this return. Not the re-routed line's dotted part"), and the owner ruled the report "Yes, as sketched". The coder also disclosed the reading in the PR body and asked for it to be checked. So this is **not** a departure from the ruling; it is a place where the ruled report and the owner's sentence can be read two ways, and the report's own step paths show the tension: 3.3 step 3 says the 50 m re-route is "Still quiet", 3.4 says the alert fires when far from the pre-Return track and the trail lines, and the walker above satisfies both.

One part of the coder's reason is overstated: "a stray could never read as far from [the whole line]" (ReturnWatch.kt:121-123, PR body). A re-routed line is fixed once shown, so a walker who keeps straying is more than 40 m from its start after another 40 m; the whole-line reading covers the stray already walked, not a stray that continues. That weakens the argument but not the conclusion, since the conclusion is 8.4 as ruled.

**Question for the owner:** on a way home, after a re-route, is the dotted line back along your own stray "your track"? If yes: pass `trackBack.points` as a second `alsoOn` line (one more line in AppContainer.kt:405-409 and a `ShownTrail` that carries both parts), with a test for the walker on the dotted part. If no: nothing changes. Either answer fits this PR's design; it does not need to hold the merge.

### 3. The AppContainer.kt typing, and the two watches reading each other

- **No lock cycle.** `ReturnWatch.onFix` holds `ReturnWatch.lock` (ReturnWatch.kt:263) when it calls `takeTrailShown` (:272) and so `wayHomeTrail` (:355), which in production reads `wayHomeWatch.shown.value` (AppContainer.kt:406): `StateFlow.value`, no monitor. `WayHomeWatch.onFix` holds `WayHomeWatch.lock` (WayHomeWatch.kt:124) when it calls `isReturning` (:128), which reads `returnWatch.state.value` (AppContainer.kt:444): the same, no monitor. `WayHomeWatch.progressFor` (:118-120) is the same shape. Neither path takes the other watch's lock, so there is no order to get wrong. The doc comment at ReturnWatch.kt:116-117 states the constraint on `wayHomeTrail` ("must not take a lock of its own"), which is the right place for it.
- **Start-up order.** `returnWatch` is initialised at AppContainer.kt:398 and `wayHomeWatch` at :439, so `returnWatch` first. The lambda at :405 references the `wayHomeWatch` field but dereferences it only when called, and it is called only from `onFix` while returning. Were a fix to arrive during the container's construction the field would still be null and the call would throw; I did not trace how the service obtains the container, so "it cannot" is inferred from the container being built in `Application`, not read. The comment at :401-403 records this reasoning.
- **Why both are typed explicitly.** The first batch of runs failed to compile: job log of 40171062417, `AppContainer.kt:443:31 Type checking has run into a recursive problem. Easiest workaround: specify the types of your declarations explicitly`, then unresolved `state` and type-parameter errors on the same line. Each watch's initialiser reads the other's type, so inference of either needs the other. `: ReturnWatch` and `: WayHomeWatch` (AppContainer.kt:398, :439) are the stated fix. The dispatch said "one argument in AppContainer.kt"; the diff is one argument plus two type annotations, which is the least change that compiles, and the coder disclosed it (finding 399-3).
