# Closed decisions

These are the content of the document. Each is attributed. The owner's words were said in the planner session on 2026-09-26.

**Owner decisions**
- **O1.** "we do need to redesign the sideways feature … how about you design the sideways layout?" Scope: "The whole app in landscape". Problem: "Looks broken" and "Not designed at all".
- **O2.** "Keep the icon strip at the port side. That's my design." The owner clarified this means the bottom nav bar. On a sideways phone the bottom navigation bar becomes a vertical navigation rail on the charger-port side, so it swaps sides between the two landscape rotations.
- **O3.** A sideways phone gets "The redesign, adapted": the compact redesign, with layout chosen by height as well as width. Tablets and foldables keep the wide tree unchanged.
- **O4.** "You're the designer so pick something based on your mobile UX research." The planner decides everything below.
- **O5.** The camera following the phone's orientation with auto-rotate off, and its effect on `user_rotation`, are by design. "Lock camera to portrait" is the way to disable it.

**Planner decisions, under O4**
- **P1. Which windows count as short.**
  - A window shorter than 480dp is short, whatever its width. That is Material 3's compact-height boundary.
  - A short window uses the compact tree, adapted as below.
  - Every other window keeps the existing rule: `COMPACT` below 600dp wide, wide tree otherwise.
  - The mechanism is a new function or class beside `WindowWidthClass`, not a conditional threaded through existing code (CLAUDE.md, Building).
- **P2. Which side is the port side.**
  - The port side comes from display rotation, using the same rotation-to-edge mapping `cameraArrangement` uses.
  - ROTATION_90 puts the port on the right; ROTATION_270 puts it on the left. This matches where the system navigation bar sits, as the capture observed.
  - Portrait is unchanged: the bottom bar stays on the port edge.
- **P3. The rail.**
  - Material 3 `NavigationRail`, standard 80dp, on the port side, next to the system navigation bar.
  - It holds the same destinations as the bottom navigation bar, in the same order, top to bottom.
  - The rail's header slot holds a Search action.
  - The rail takes the `navigationBars` inset on its side.
- **P4. The punch-hole side.**
  - No interactive element sits within the `displayCutout` inset.
  - Map tiles may draw under it; controls are padded by it.
- **P5. The top edge.**
  - The status bar stays visible.
  - No full-width bars in landscape. Every top element is sized to its content.
  - Reason: height is the scarce axis (see Prior art).
- **P6. The icon cluster.**
  - In landscape its default side is the punch-hole side, inside the cut-out inset.
  - Drag, snap and minimise are unchanged.
  - Its position, side and minimised flag are remembered separately for portrait and for landscape, so turning the phone never discards what the user set. That follows CLAUDE.md UX defaults.
  - Persistence is within the session only, the same as today.
- **P7. The compass/elevation strip.** Top centre of the map area, sized to its content rather than `fillMaxWidth`. It stays a plain Box that passes touches through.
- **P8. Search.**
  - The rail's Search action opens a side sheet anchored to the rail side.
  - The sheet is 360dp wide, or the available width if smaller, and full height below the status bar.
  - It holds the search field and its results/dropdown.
  - It closes on Back, on the Search action again, or on a scrim tap.
  - There is no search bar over the map in landscape.
- **P9. The navigation HUD.**
  - Docked at the top of the map area, on the rail side, at most 360dp wide.
  - While it shows, the compass strip centres in the remaining width.
- **P10. Fullscreen.**
  - Hides the rail, sliding it toward the port edge. The system navigation bar remains.
  - Entry, exit and Back behave as in portrait.
  - The persisted fullscreen flag is unchanged.
- **P11. Other destinations.**
  - List, Seasonal, the Journal tabs and Records sub-tabs, log entry view and edit, Cartography, Album/Gallery and Settings lay out beside the rail.
  - Content is capped at `READABLE_CONTENT_MAX_WIDTH` (640dp), centred in the remaining width, and scrolls vertically.
- **P12. The tools drawer.**
  - Opens from the rail side.
  - Closes in one action on a scrim tap or on Back, in both orientations. This fixes both findings above, because the drawer is one component. Record it as a bug fix that applies to portrait too.
- **P13. Unchanged.** The photo viewer (already full-window, with controls on `safeDrawing`) and the in-app camera (it has its own landscape arrangement, and O5 applies).
- **P14. Departure from the platform default.**
  - Android's `NavigationSuiteScaffold` default shows a bottom navigation bar whenever height is compact.
  - This design uses a side rail instead.
  - Reasons: O2; Material's navigation-rail guidance for phones wider than 600dp in landscape; the map-app evidence that stacked horizontal bars squash the map in landscape; and the system's own 3-button bar sitting on the same port edge.
- **P15. What this fixes by construction.** One tree in both orientations removes two failures the code pulse found:
  - a navigation started in portrait has no stop/exit control after rotation;
  - portrait state is carried into the wide tree, where the UI can't show it (for example a persisted fullscreen flag making Back toggle an invisible state).

  The pulse found these by reading `AvailabilityScreen.kt:771-783`, `:896-943` and `:2915-3126`. They were not run; say so.
