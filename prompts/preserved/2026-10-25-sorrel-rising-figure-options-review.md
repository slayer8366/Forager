## 3. Attacking B's never-short claim (the Blocking finding, B-1)

B moves the figure forward whenever a fix lands within 10 m of a later stretch. So it reads short whenever a fix wrong by more than 10 m lands on a part of the line that comes **later** than the walker's. The write-up shows this once, on H, and says it costs "one fix". Three things are missing.

**(a) It is S's own exposure, not just the hairpin's.** On S the wander comes first in the line and the trail, 30 m north of it, comes later. A walker on the wander at (150,−30) (truth 500) whose fix is displaced north by d m (replica):

| d | 5 | 10 | 15 | 19 | **20** | **25** | **30** | **35** | **40** | 41 | 45 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | 514 | 505 | 514 | 514 | **205** | **205** | **215** | **205** | **205** | 543 | 533 | 524 |
| vs truth | +14 | +5 | +14 | +14 | **−295** | **−295** | **−285** | **−295** | **−295** | +43 | +33 | +24 |

Errors of 20 to 40 m toward the trail land on it; the figure jumps from about 500 to about 205. At 41 m and beyond the fix lands on nothing and is over 25 m from the confirmed stretch, so the 60 m rule takes over and reads long. **The exposure window is a 20 m band, not the quarter-metre sliver B-395-2 was.** By leg separation d, the window is about [max(11, d − 10), min(d + 10, 50)] m of error toward the later part: legs 10 m apart, 11 to 20 m; 20 m apart, 11 to 29 m; 30 m apart (S), 20 to 39 m; 50 m apart, 41 to 50 m (replica sweep on the hairpin with the riser at each separation). So on the tight switchbacks `TrackSelfJoin.kt:22-28` was built for, a fix 11 m wrong reads short. The baseline reads long on every one of these.

**(b) "One fix" assumes the next fix is right, and GPS errors are not independent.** Position errors under canopy are multipath, and multipath bias persists for seconds to minutes. A 25 m northward bias held while the walker goes from (150,−30) to (200,−30) (replica): B reads 205, 205, 225, 225, 245, 245 against truths 500 to 450, **short by 205 to 295 m for the whole stretch**, recovering (456 against 440) only when the bias clears. The HUD samples the watch's progress every 5 s (`ROUTE_TICK_MILLIS`, `TrackRecordingViewModel.kt:861`) and sundown every 15 s (`SUNDOWN_TICK_MILLIS`, `TrackRecordingService.kt:551`), so a one-fix excursion may or may not be seen, and a sustained one is. For sundown the effect of a short reading is that an alert due in that window is not raised until the figure recovers: one tick late for a one-fix error, as long as the bias for a sustained one.

**(c) How likely.** This is a claim about position error, and the one sample is of *reported* accuracy, not measured error (`LiveFixGate.kt:73-74`: "whether it is calibrated… is not known"). Android reports accuracy as a 68% radius, so a fix reporting 20 m is more than 20 m wrong about a third of the time by definition; taking that as a Rayleigh with 68% radius 20 m, the northward component lies in [20, 40] m about 6 to 7% of the time, so roughly one fix in fifteen among those reporting 20 m, while the walker is on a part of the line that has a later part 30 m to one side. For a fix reporting 3.79 m the chance is negligible *if* that figure is honest; `NavigationReadout.kt:29-39` records that on the S22 it was once a placeholder, 289 of 289, and `LiveFixGate.kt:68-69` that outdoors it is now that phone's best case, not a constant. `NavigationReadout.kt:22-23`: "GPS accuracy under canopy is routinely 10–20 m." I cannot turn this into a rate for the owner's walks; what can be said is that B's shortness is not a boundary curiosity, it is a band of ordinary canopy error, and it lasts as long as the error does.

**The mirror case is a flicker, not a safety issue.** A trail-walker on S at (150,0) whose fix is displaced 20 to 40 m south toward the wander hits the turned-back rule and reads 505 to 553 against 170 (long by 335 to 383) for that fix. Under the baseline the figure is already about 550 there, so B is no worse, but B's "never climbs beside your wander" has this exception: one wrong fix shows the climb for a tick.

**Stale fixes, jumps, re-routes.** As Juniper says: a stale fix leaves the figure where it was (no `onFix`), and `ReturnWalkingTime.kt:78, 95` degrade and withhold as before; a jump within the gate is case (a) if it lands on a later part; a re-route or line swap clears progress and `lastPlace` (`WayHomeWatch.kt:197-198`) so B restarts under the 60 m rule until a fix lands, which on a new line that starts at the most recent stored point is the first fix. Confirmed from the code; nothing new found there.

**A shares the exposure**, as Juniper says in principle, with S figures now: a stored point 25 or 30 m north of the wander at (150,−30) joins the outbound trail and the track figure reads **175 or 170 against 500**, and at the next stored point 202 (replica; 20 m and 40 m miss the join by float, the same boundary as H). The sampler stores a point every 15 s or 15 m on BALANCED (`TrackRecordingMode.kt:28`), so A has fewer chances per minute than B and each one persists for about 90 m of walking.

## 5. §1's argument, and a candidate it missed

The theorem holds under its own model: if a fix may be anywhere within 50 m of the walker and nothing else is known, a walker on the wander 30 m south of the trail produces every fix sequence a trail-walker does, so no rule, with or without history, can read lower than the earliest part within reach and stay never short. That is right, and C is its demonstration.

What is missed is that **the gate's 50 m is a ceiling on the fix's *reported* accuracy, and each fix carries its own**. A fifth candidate, call it E: the reach is this fix's reported accuracy times a factor, plus the 10 m node step, floored and capped at 60 m; today's rule otherwise unchanged, stateless, one line. On S with a fix reporting 3.79 m and a factor of 2 or 3, the reach is 20 m and the figure runs 20 to 45 m long with no climb; reporting 10 m, reach 30, still no climb; reporting 15 m or more, reach 40 or more, it climbs exactly as today, which is honest (replica). E is never short exactly when the true error is within the factor times the reported figure, and it degrades to today's rule by itself when the phone says it is unsure, which is the Don't-make-me-think shape: no state, no hold band, no recovery. **It has a standing objection in the code**, which is why it must be written up rather than dropped: `LiveFixGate.kt:73-75` ("the advice stands not to build an uncertainty calibration on it"), `NavigationReadout.kt:38-39` ("do not build on this threshold as if it tracked fix quality"), `ReturnWalkingTime.kt:50-51` ("this estimate takes no dependency on it"); against that, `hasArrived` already decides from it by the owner's word ("Twice accuracy, at least 15 m"). Whether the distance home may read the reported accuracy is an owner's ruling, not a researcher's, and the write-up should put it beside the 10 m question rather than leave the owner with three levers when there are four. I do not recommend E over B; I note that B's never-short standard (a fixed 10 m, from a constant whose own doc says "This is not yet the real number… Beta data decides", `TrackSelfJoin.kt:28-30`) is not better founded than E's.

## 6. The rulings §8 asks for

Replace the H case with a fix that lands (e.g. (100,25): 235 against 420) so the exposure is shown honestly, and note that on a 20 m hairpin a fix 11 m wrong already lands.
