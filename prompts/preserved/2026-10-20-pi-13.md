## Why

The build checks that each sample point has a routing square, and that the square isn't
implausibly small. It never asks BRouter for a route. So "routing follows trails" is unverified.

This data is also the first built in bands. Squares on either side of a band edge were built by
separate BRouter runs. Canada's two-band test showed the squares came out byte-identical to a whole
build, but no route has crossed a band edge on real data.

## Routes to run, each between two named places on one named trail

Choose real trails with clear OSM names. For each, record:

- which band(s) it falls in;
- whether it crosses a band edge;
- why you chose it.

At least five routes:

- **A. Ramona Falls, Oregon.** Use the same names as `verify-orwa.sh`'s own example: "Ramona Falls
  Trailhead" to "Ramona Falls", trail text "Ramona". Compare with the Oregon + Washington result if
  the record holds one: search `docs/audits/` for the pi-build part 1 report's routing check.
- **B. A trail in Canada,** for example in the Rockies or Ontario.
- **C. A trail in Alaska or Hawaii.**
- **D. At least one route that crosses a band edge:** a longitude in −125, −120, −110, −100, −95,
  −90, −85, −80 or −75. One near −80 or −85 is worth having, because those squares are the densest.
- **E. A short urban park trail,** such as Forest Park in Portland.

For each, export the ways near the route for `route_on_named_ways.py` with a small
`osmium extract -b` and `osmium export -f geojsonseq`, into scratch only. Then report:

- whether a route was produced;
- its length;
- its share on the named trail, from `route_on_named_ways.py --name-contains`;
- its length by highway type.

**Also run a positive control.** Route between the same two places with a profile or setting that
keeps off paths, for example `trekking` or a car profile if one is present, and show the share on
the trail drops. That proves the share measure can fail. If no such profile is available, say so,
and use a route between two points the trail does not join instead.

## Privacy

`verify-orwa.sh`'s rule holds:

- coordinates, tile numbers and tracks go only into a scratch directory under `/tmp`, which you
  remove at the end;
- the report carries names, lengths, shares and counts, never positions;
- no IP addresses or network names.

## Prediction and finish line

- **Prediction (the planner's):**
  - every route is produced;
  - A to C and E run 80% or more of their length on the named trail;
  - the band-edge route has no gap at the edge, its track continuous across it, and its length
    within 10% of the trail's own length;
  - the positive control's share is clearly lower.
- **Finish line:** a report on each route, every claim marked measured, read or inferred, with the
  command and time. The prediction is scored right or wrong.

## Stops

Stop and report, never choose:

- any write outside your scratch directory;
- touching units, the timer, `/opt` contents, `/etc` or the card's files;
- a route that fails in a way that suggests the squares are wrong. Report it with BRouter's own
  message, stripped of coordinates;
- a refusal by the permission system. Do not route around it.
