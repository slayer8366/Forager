## Phase 1, part B: prior art, recorded

For each part of the map, look at three or four established maps and record:

- what they do;
- what the convention protects;
- whether there are two camps.

The parts:

- trails by difficulty (`sac_scale`), visibility (`trail_visibility`) and surface;
- informal trails;
- land and water colours;
- woods and parks;
- contours, if any;
- road hierarchy;
- labels;
- points of interest.

Candidates to look at include OpenStreetMap's standard style, OpenTopoMap, Waymarked Trails Hiking,
a national topo map such as swisstopo or the USGS topo, and the hiking apps people know (AllTrails,
Gaia GPS, CalTopo), from their public screenshots or documentation.

Name every source, with its URL and the date read. Describe other products generically in anything
committed, as CLAUDE.md asks; names are fine in this report.

## Phase 1, part C: the proposal for the owner

Write a short proposal in behaviour terms: what a person sees at a glance, and at which zoom. Group
it into the few decisions that are the owner's, each with two or three options and a recommendation.

- **The palette:** keep Protomaps light, a warmer field-map look, or a high-contrast outdoor look.
- **How trail difficulty shows:** colour, dash pattern, or both.
- **What hides:** for example shops and other POIs, which matter little to foraging.
- **Labels.**
- **What each choice costs:** in the scaffold's override file alone (colours and hides), or as new
  generator rules. Styling by `sac_scale` needs rules the override file can't express.

Show the leading options on the local preview where it is cheap. That means variant styles in
scratch, not in the repo, each with a screenshot.

Say how the style reaches users, read from the code:

- the Worker's `src/offline-style.json`;
- the app's own style files;
- which of them Rowan's side must change.
