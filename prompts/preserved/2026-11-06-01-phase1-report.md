## 1. Which lines carry ways, and what they know

Common to every trail line [read]:

- **BRouter's turn instructions are off.**
  - The request passes no `turnInstructionMode` (`BRouterTrailRouter.kt:143-160`).
  - The profile's `turnInstructionMode = 1` ("auto", `hiking-mountain.brf:57`) is never applied (`RoutingContext.java:177-180`).
  - Detours are registered only when the mode is above 0 (`RoutingEngine.java:2191`).
  - So `processVoiceHints` returns early (`OsmTrack.java:457`).

- **Junctions can be found.** `BRouterRouteWays` (`routing/BRouterRouteWays.kt:59-119`) decodes every link round a point. A route point that is a link end shared by 3 or more links is a junction. ## 2. A turn against a winding path, from real data

**Angle alone cannot tell them apart** [observed, run 42214685039, counted once]:
- Bends within one way (peak change over 20 m, no junction): 1,097 of 30° or more (5.8 per km), 244 of 60° or more, and 53 of 90° or more.
- Manoeuvres where the route leaves the straight-on way: 456. 297 of them turn less than 90°.
- So any angle threshold either counts bends or drops turns.

**The way's tags cannot say "the way it is on"** [observed]:
- The tags are unchanged across 120 of the 456 manoeuvres that leave the straight-on way.
- They change across 298 of the 680 manoeuvres that keep it.

**What separates them is the other ways at the junction.** At 20 m (rows: the route's turn):

| Route's turn | 0-10 | 10-20 | 20-30 | 30-45 | 45-60 | 60-90 | 90-135 | 135-180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Route keeps the straightest way (680) | 249 | 122 | 85 | 106 | 57 | 57 | 4 | 0 |
| Route leaves it, another way is straighter (456) | 1 | 7 | 18 | 49 | 71 | 151 | 126 | 33 |

"Keeps the straightest" with a large turn includes T-junctions approached on the stem, where the route is the straightest by a hair: 33 manoeuvres bend 60-120° and are the straightest by less than 20°. There is no straight-on way to carry on along there.

**Proposed rule R** [inferred from the data above]:
1. **Junctions only.** A turn is only at a node where 3 or more ways meet in the route data. A bend within a way is never a turn (the owner: "not winding paths").
2. **How the angle is measured.** The route's direction comes from the point 20 m back along the route to the node, and from the node to the point 20 m on. Each other way's direction runs from the node to 20 m along it, or to its end if it is shorter.
3. **When it is a turn.** Either another way is straighter than the route's, or the route bends at least **S = 30°** and is the straightest by less than **M = 30°**.
4. **Ways counted.** Every way in the data counts, including private and motorway. 31 manoeuvres have such a branch, and ignoring them changes the class of 12.

**Counts of R** (1,136 manoeuvres):

| S | M = 0 | M = 10 | M = 20 | M = 30 | M = 45 | M = 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 456 | 475 | 516 | 544 | 577 | 605 |

- Neither the angle nor the margin has a valley. The margin is a judgement, and the table prices it.
- R(30, 30) gives 1.83 turns per km walked, against 4.02 junction passes per km.
- 4 of the 200 routes have no turn.

**Why 20 m:**
- The class is the same at 10, 20 and 30 m for 1,016 of 1,136 manoeuvres. Moving from 10 to 20 m changes 67, and from 20 to 30 m changes 55 [observed].
- 20 m is the hiking profile's own `turnInstructionCatchingRange` (`hiking-mountain.brf:58`) [read].
- It sits under the needle's 25 m lookahead [read].
- 14% of junction-to-junction gaps are under 20 m (481 of 3,503) [observed].

**Forks where the route keeps the "same" path** [observed]:
- 70 manoeuvres keep the straightest way with another way leaving within 45° of it. In 51 of them the route itself bends under 20°.
- Under R they are not turns: carrying straight on, as the planner's reading has it.

**BRouter's own instructions, compared with R** [observed]:
- BRouter hints a turn at 490 of R's 544.
- It hints 218 more that R leaves out, 181 of them "slight". 117 of those bend under 30°: bends at a junction.

**Dog-legs.** 90 of 1,490 turn-to-turn gaps along a route are under 20 m [observed].

## 4. The walker's place, the distance, and when a turn is passed

**Where the place is computed** [read]:
- **The place** (`WayHomeProgress.kt:156`): the earliest node within 25 m, never before the last place. It never moves back, except when the walker turns back.
- **The figure's node** (`:165`): the earliest node within 60 m anywhere on the line, for the top line.
- **The needle's place** (`aimFrom`, `WayHomeAhead.kt:36`, `:57-71`): the nearest node in the 50 m after the place.
- **The track home** (`RouteHome.kt:178`): the first point at least the hop along the track.
- **The line's nodes.** `WayHomeLine` keeps every route point as a node, filled at 10 m steps (`WayHomeProgress.kt:59-68`). Its metres along are scaled to the parts' own metres. So the distance to a turn is `along[turnNode] - along[from]`.
- **Mapping turns onto nodes** should be by index, not coordinate equality. A filled point is computed, so it can differ from the route's by a rounding step [inferred].
- **RouteLookahead** is the needle's corner guard (`RouteLookahead.kt:18-28`), not a place along the route.

**Options for where the distance counts from:**
- **A. The place.** The turn is never passed early. But it reads up to about 25 m long: about 80 ft standing at the junction. It switches up to about 25 m after the turn [inferred from the 25 m reach].
- **B. The needle's place.** It reads true, within the fix error and the 10 m step. But it can pass early on a forward fix error. And it does not only move forward, so it flicks across the turn's node unless held.
- **C. Recommended:** the figure from the needle's place, held at 0 or above, and the turn passed only when the place passes its node.
  - The arrow and the needle then read from one place.
  - The turn never passes early.
  - No new number is needed: the place's only-forward rule is the existing hysteresis.
  - It reads 0 for up to about 25 m after the turn.

**Other existing rules that carry over** [read]:
- The far band, out above 50 m and back only below 45 m (`PathHome.kt:132-143`, `:185-188`), withholds the route. The next turn goes with it.
- A stray re-route (`WayHomeWatch.kt:177-198`) gives a new line, and the turns are recomputed on it.
- The way home's card updates on a 5 s tick (`TrackRecordingRouteTicks.kt:103-112`). The way to a waypoint updates on every fix (`AvailabilityWaypointRoute.kt:198-216`).
- **Units** follow the card's: `formatDistanceMeters` (`DistanceUnit.kt:72`), the top line's own formatter for a route.

## 8. Premises that are wrong

1. **"Leaves the way it is on for another way"** cannot be read from the data: there is no way identity, and the tags mislead in both directions (see 2). The turn has to be defined by the other ways' directions at the junction.
2. **"Carrying straight on … is never a turn"** needs a straight-on way to exist. At a T approached on its stem (33 manoeuvres), the route is technically the straightest, and there is none.
3. **Turns that are not at a junction.** Some direction changes have no route-data junction at all. On the way to a waypoint: where the lead-in reaches the trail, and where the route leaves the trail for the final leg. On the way home: the two joints (`FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:91`). The walker must choose left or right there.
4. **The walker's own track** (the track home, the way home's track parts, and the track fallback past the far band at `TrackRecordingRouteTicks.kt:113`) has no junction data. "No angled turn left" is unknowable there.
5. **A plotted route being navigated** is not on pre-main.
6. **"Read #498's branch for the layout it will have."** The branch doesn't hold G2, G3, H1 or H2 yet. The room in 6 was measured before them, and H's reading may narrow it.
7. **"The Worker serves it" around P.** Not tried. CI's unit-test step has no app key (`ci.yml`, the build step's note), so the routing fixture was used.

## 9. Options for the owner

Each option below has a recommendation and the reason for it.

- **O1. Which junctions count.**
  - R(30°, 30°), 544 turns: **recommended**. It is the smallest rule that catches a T approached on its stem and a wide Y.
  - R with M = 0, 456 turns: misses those.
  - BRouter's own instructions: adds 218, mostly slight bends at junctions.
- **O2. Forks where the route keeps straight on** (70).
  - Not a turn: **recommended, the planner's reading**.
  - A "keep" arrow (ForkLeft/ForkRight).
- **O3. The part joins.**
  - **Recommended:** count them by the same rule, with the trail's two directions as the ways there. Choosing the way along the trail is often the walk's first decision.
  - Or: not turns.
- **O4. U-turn.**
  - **Recommended:** sharp covers 135-180°, the three bands the owner named (none of the sample turns is 170° or more).
  - Or: a U-turn arrow from 170°.
- **O5. Where the distance counts from and when a turn is passed:** C, **recommended** (see 4).
- **O6. States the rulings don't cover:** "Arrived", the dash, the withheld route, a lost fix, within the fix's circle, and the approximate or last-known position.
  - **Recommended:** the space keeps its place, blank, wherever the needle is withheld or the top line holds a word or a dash. The straight-ahead arrow shows otherwise.
- **O7. The walker's own track.**
  - Blank: **recommended**. The data can't say there is no turn, and CLAUDE.md asks for "unsupported", never a plausible stand-in.
  - Or: Q3's straight-ahead arrow with the distance home.
  - The blank could read as broken, so check it on the device.
- **O8. TalkBack words** for the arrow, such as "left in 1.1 mi": the owner's words.
- **O9. Dog-legs.**
  - **Recommended:** each turn in its turn, with no merging; the data shows the case but not how it reads.
  - Or: merge turns within 20 m into one.
- **O10. The junction source** (the planner's call).
  - RouteWays along the route: **recommended**. The interface is the project's own and already in production.
  - BRouter's hints: these need its package-private fields, which means editing the vendored tree, kept unmodified (`third_party/brouter/README.md`).
- **O11. Q6's fit:** options (a) to (e) in 6, not chosen.
