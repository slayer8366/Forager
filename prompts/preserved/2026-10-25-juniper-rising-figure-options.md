## 0. The one-paragraph version

The figure climbs because the rule reads it from the earliest point of the line that the walker *could* be at, given that a fix may be 50 m wrong. Where the route runs within reach of its own earlier part, that earliest point is on the earlier part, and no rule that trusts a fix only as far as the gate does can tell the two apart (§1). So the real choice is **how far to trust a fix**: the gate's 50 m (the current rule, which climbs), or the 10 m the app already trusts stored points to when it joins a track to itself (candidates A and B, which do not climb on Sorrel's case). I recommend **B, "follow your progress along the line"**: the figure moves forward only when a fix lands on a later stretch of the line, moves back when a fix lands on an earlier one, holds while a fix wanders up to 25 m off, and falls back to today's rule beyond that. On Sorrel's out-and-back it reads 65 to 75 m at the car instead of 630 m; on an ordinary trail it reads 25 to 35 m long instead of 60 to 85 m; and it is never short to the same standard today's track figure is never short to. Its one exposure, which the owner must accept knowingly, is a fix wrong by more than 10 m that lands on a later part of the line, which reads short for one fix (§4.3).

## 1. Why the figure climbs, and why no single-fix rule can stop it

**What is built (confirmed, `WayHomeProgress.kt:124-144` at `77963f8a4`).** For each fix two things are found on the way-home line. The *place*, which the needle and the greyed walked part read, is the earliest node not before the last place, within 25 m of the fix, else within 50 m, else anywhere (`:130-134`). The *figure* ignores the last place: it is the line's metres from the earliest node **anywhere** within the reach of the fix (`:139-142`), plus 25 m when that node is on the trail part. The reach at this commit is 50 m (`:160`); Amendment 4 widens it to 60 m, the gate's 50 m plus the line's 10 m node step (RECORD -1620). That build is not at `77963f8a4`; the figures below use 60 m as ruled. The figure reaches the HUD as `routeMeters` (`WayHomeAhead.kt:39`) and sundown through `wayHomeFor` (`AppContainer.kt:449`, `SundownWatch.kt:296-297`), the same number both places.

**Why that rule is right under its own premise.** The gate admits a fix with a reported accuracy up to 50 m (`LiveFixGate.kt:78`). If the walker is somewhere on the line and the fix is within 50 m of them, then the line's node just behind them is within 60 m of the fix. So the earliest node within 60 m of the fix is at or before the walker, and the metres from it are never short. That argument uses one fix and nothing else, and it is tight: of all the places on the line a 50 m-wrong fix could have come from, the rule reads the one furthest from home. The owner accepted its cost on a trail that stays away from itself, "about 50 to 75 m long" at the 50 m reach (RECORD -1618); at 60 m, re-derived, it is **60 to 85 m long** with an accurate fix (the earliest node within 60 m behind a walker on a straight line is 50 to 60 m behind them, plus the 25 m allowance).

**Why it climbs (Sorrel's M-395-5, re-derived).** The walker goes 300 m east along a trail from the trailhead, steps off, forages back west 30 m south of the trail, and taps Return 20 m east of the trailhead. The way home is their wander back east (280 m, then 30 m north to the trail: 310 m), the trail west (300 m), their track to the car (20 m): 630 m. Walking the trail home, a fix at (x, 0) is within 60 m of every wander node within 52 m east or west of x, and the wander comes *before* the trail in the line. So the figure counts from the wander:

| Walker on the trail, m east of the trailhead | True distance home | Figure (60 m reach) | Long by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 290 | 310 | about 410 | 100 |
| 200 | 220 | about 500 | 280 |
| 100 | 120 | about 600 | 480 |
| 72 and below, to the trailhead | 92 to 20 | 630 | 540 to 610 |
| 15 from the car | | "Arrived" | |

The figure rises by about 220 m while the walker covers 290 m toward the car, reads the whole route for the last 72 m, and then flips to "Arrived" (straight-line, within the larger of 15 m and twice the fix's accuracy; `NavigationReadout.kt:67-71`, read by sundown at `SundownWatch.kt:288-294`). Sundown reads the same 630 m as walk-back time for those last 72 m. With the 50 m reach the table is Sorrel's (400, 490, 590, 630 from 60 m in).

**Why no rule can fix this at the gate's trust level (inferred, and I think it is a theorem).** A fix at (100, 0) is consistent, within 50 m, with a walker on the trail at 100 m and with a walker on the wander at anything from 48 m to 152 m east. To read lower than 600, the rule has to rule out the wander. The fix cannot. Nor can the history of fixes: the wander and the trail are 30 m apart, so every sequence of fixes that fits "walking west on the trail" also fits "walking west along the wander, 30 m south, with each fix 30 m off". Nothing in the gate model forbids that. So every rule that keeps the gate's 50 m as the only thing it trusts, however it uses history, must climb exactly where the current one does, wherever the earlier part is within 60 m. Candidate C below is the best such history rule and §5 shows it either climbs or reads short.

**What this leaves.** Three levers, and only three:
1. **Trust the fix more than the gate does**, to a standard the project already uses. The app joins a track to itself where two stored points are within 10 m (`TrackSelfJoin.kt:32`), and the owner's ruling there is that stepping across 10 m "is not meaningfully different from standing where the walker already stood", valid "only while the gap it bridges is within GPS error of the walked path anyway" (`:13-20`). The track figure sundown reads before Return, and falls back to after, is never short *to that standard*. Candidates A and B use it.
2. **Keep the climb and change what sundown or the HUD does with it.** Candidate D.
3. **Keep the current rule.** The baseline.

Numbers in §2 to §6 use four cases: **S**, Sorrel's out-and-back above; **O**, an ordinary trail home on new ground, 500 m, nowhere near its own earlier part; **H**, the test fixture's hairpin (`WayHomeLines.kt:16-24`: track 50 m north to the trail at (0, 0), trail east 200 m, north 20 m, west 200 m to (0, 20), track 100 m north; 570 m), with Sorrel's B-395-1 fix (walker truly at (100, 0) on leg 1, fix at (100, 30), nearer leg 2); **T**, the double-back on that hairpin (walker reaches the riser at (200, 0) and turns back west along leg 1); and **P**, Sorrel's B-395-2 shape 2 (legs 70 m apart, walker at (105, 0), fix at (105, 49.9) at the gate's edge).

## 2. Baseline: keep the current rule

**Rule in plain words.** The distance home counts from the earliest point of the whole route that is within 60 m of your fix, plus 25 m on the trail part. "The furthest from home you could possibly be."

**Step paths.**
- **S:** as the table in §1. Tap Return 20 m from the car → 630 m. Walk your wander back east → the figure falls to about 330 m where you reach the trail. Walk the trail west → it *rises*: 410, 500, 600, then 630 m from 72 m out to the trailhead. 15 m from the car → "Arrived". Sundown reads the same.
- **O:** 500 m at Return, falls steadily, always 60 to 85 m long; 85 m from the car when you reach it, then "Arrived".
- **H:** the fix at (100, 30): the earliest leg-1 node within 60 m is (50, 0); 570 − 100 + 25 = **495 m** against 420. Long by 75. (Sorrel's 485 is the same case at 50 m.)
- **T:** turning back east along leg 1: at (200, 0) 405 against 320; at (100, 0) 505 against 420; at (0, 0) 570 against 520. Long by 50 to 85 all the way.
- **P:** the earliest leg-1 node within 60 m of (105, 49.9) is (80, 0): 620 − 130 + 25 = **515 m** against 465. Long by 50. (This is the case Amendment 4 closes; at 50 m it read 275.)

**Never short?** Yes, for every fix the gate admits, by the argument in §1. This is its whole merit and it is real.

**What it costs the walker.** On S, the number grows as they walk toward the car and reads the full route beside it; then "Arrived" replaces it. That is the state CLAUDE.md names as the worst: one the user can misread as broken. Sundown may say "leave by" or raise the sunset alert at the car with 630 m of walk-back in hand. On O, 60 to 85 m long throughout, which the owner accepted. How often S happens: whenever the forager walks home along a trail that runs within 60 m of their own outbound wander, which is the ordinary forager pattern Sorrel named.

**Size.** Nothing beyond Amendment 4 (already dispatched).

## 3. Candidate A: the lower of two never-short figures

**Rule in plain words.** Two distances home exist after Return: the way home's (today's rule, §2) and the track's (the figure sundown read before Return: along your own recorded track, joined to itself wherever it passes within 10 m of itself, from your most recent stored point; `PathHome.kt:98-116`, `TrackSelfJoin.kt`). Show and use the **lower**. Your return walk is being recorded too, so the track figure keeps up with where you have actually been.

**Step paths.**
- **S:** Return → both say about 630 m. Walking the wander back east, your new points land on your old wander points (within 10 m), so the track joins there and both figures agree, falling to about 330 m at the trail. Walking the trail west, your new points land on your outbound trail points, the track joins, and the track figure is about x + 20 m (the trail west of you, plus the car leg). The way-home figure climbs to 630; the lower is the track's: about **310, 220, 120, 40 m**, then "Arrived". Where a stretch of return-walk points misses the outbound points by more than 10 m (GPS 10 to 20 m off, which the S22 shows outdoors on a minority of fixes, `LiveFixGate.kt:68-69`), the track route runs back along your return walk to the last join before heading home, so the figure reads the metres since that join extra, then drops when the next join forms: a small sawtooth, tens of metres.
- **O:** the track figure is the return walk back to Return plus the whole track home, which grows as you walk; the way-home figure falls. The lower is the way home's: **as the baseline, 60 to 85 m long.**
- **H:** the fix at (100, 30) is also the most recent stored point, if the sampler keeps it. It lies within 10 m of outbound leg-2 points (if the walker's outbound track was this trail), so the track joins there: 10 m across, 100 m west on leg 2, 100 m north: **about 210 m against 420**. The lower is 210. **Short by 210.** This is the self-join doing exactly what its ruling says it may not be trusted for: a 30 m-wrong point bridging a 20 m gap.
- **T:** both figures never short (the track one by the owner's most-recent-point ruling, `PathHome.kt:22-24`); the lower is fine.
- **P:** the way-home figure 515 (baseline); the track figure from a point 49.9 m off the trail: hop 49.9 m counted as far, plus the track home; the lower is 515 or thereabouts. Never short.

**Never short?** Only to the self-join's standard: a stored point within 10 m of where the walker stood. Under the gate's 50 m it is short wherever a wrong point creates a false join (**H**), and the false join **persists**: as the walker goes on east along leg 1, the shortest track route still runs back along their return walk to the bad point and across the join, so it reads short until they have walked about 105 m past it (inferred: after d metres more along leg 1 the route via the bad point is 210 + d, the truth 420 − d, and the first is lower while d < 105). Also, where the trail route crosses or runs within 10 m of your outbound track somewhere midway, and your track from that crossing is the shorter way home, the figure reads the track's distance **while the line drawn is the trail's**: a number below the line it is shown with. The track's distance is a real way home you walked, so it is not short against the ground; it is short against the route shown, which is what the walker is following and what -1589 says the figure is for.

**What it costs the walker.** On S, nothing: one number, falling, right. On O, as today. At a crossing, a dip in the number then a rise as they leave the crossing, with no change in the line: "which route is this number for?" is a thought. The sawtooth under poor GPS is a smaller one.

**Size.** Small. The two places that choose between the way-home figure and the track figure would compute both and take the lower: the HUD tick (`TrackRecordingRouteTicks.kt:105-114`, 127 lines) and sundown's estimate (`SundownWatch.kt:296-297`, 456 lines, or better inside `ReturnWalkingTime.kt:77-83`, 143 lines, which already receives both the track and the path). The HUD's `routeMeters` and `pathHome` (`WayHomeAhead.kt:39, 43`) would carry the lower. No new rule, no new constant. Tests at both read points on the S fixture (new) and the H fixture. Behaviour: the line unchanged; the figure never above today's; dips at crossings; the semantics of -1589 ("the HUD distance and sundown use the route shown") bent, since the figure can be the track's while the route shown is the trail's. That is a stop-and-ask on wording, not on safety.

## 4. Candidate B: follow your progress along the line, trusting a fix that lands on it (recommended)

**Rule in plain words.** The figure keeps a *confirmed place* on the line, where you have been shown to be. It moves **forward** only when a fix lands within 10 m of a stretch of line at or after the confirmed place (the earliest such stretch). It moves **back** when no stretch at or after is within 10 m but an earlier one is (you turned back). It **holds** when the fix is more than 10 m from every stretch but still within 25 m of the confirmed one (GPS wobble, a step off the trail). Beyond 25 m of the confirmed stretch, the figure is **today's rule** (earliest point within 60 m anywhere), which also governs until the first fix lands on the line after Return or a re-route. The figure is the line's metres from the start of the confirmed stretch, plus the owner's 25 m on the trail part. The needle and the greyed part keep their own place as now; this is the figure's only.

The three figures are the project's own: 10 m is the self-join ε (`TrackSelfJoin.kt:32`), the distance the app already treats as "where you stood"; 25 m is the trail-join figure, "how close a track point must be to a trail to count as on it" (`WayHome.kt:103-110`), and the hop's enter figure (`PathHome.kt:164`); 60 m is Amendment 4's reach. No new number.

**Step paths.**
- **S:** Return at (20, −30) → the first fix is on the wander, the confirmed place is its start: **630 m**. Walking east along the wander, each fix lands on the next stretch: the figure falls, 530, 430, 330 at the trail. Onto the trail and west: each fix lands on the trail stretch beside it, which comes after the confirmed place in the line; the wander comes before it and is never looked at. The figure is the trail west of the stretch's start (up to 10 m behind you) plus 20 m plus 25 m: **335 to 345 at 290 m out; 145 to 155 at 100 m; 65 to 75 m at 20 m from the car** (truth 310, 120, 40); at the trailhead the confirmed stretch is on your track to the car, no allowance, **20 to 30 m**; at 15 m "Arrived". Falls the whole way, 25 to 35 m long. A fix 15 m off the trail (GPS): nothing within 10 m, within 25 m of the confirmed stretch → holds; the figure does not jump.
- **O:** 25 to 35 m long throughout (the stretch's start up to 10 m behind, plus 25), against today's 60 to 85.
- **H:** confirmed place on leg 1 at the stretch (90, 0)–(100, 0). Fix at (100, 30): leg 1 is 30 m away, leg 2 (100, 20) is exactly 10.0 m away, so **at the edge of the 10 m it lands on leg 2**, which is after the confirmed place: the confirmed place jumps to leg 2 and the figure reads 570 − 350 + 25 = **245 m against 420. Short by 175 for this fix.** Next fix at (101, 0): nothing at or after leg 2's (100, 20) within 10 m (leg 2 is 20 m off); leg 1 at (101, 0) is within 10 m and earlier → turned back → confirmed place moves back to leg 1: **444 m against 419**. Recovered in one fix. With the 10 m strict rather than inclusive, the first fix lands nowhere, is 31.6 m from the confirmed stretch, and falls to today's rule: 495, never short. The fixture's legs are 20 m apart, which is exactly 10 m self-join plus 10 m fix error, so it sits on B's boundary by construction; the owner should read this case as "a fix 30 m wrong, beside a part of the line 20 m away".
- **T:** at the riser the confirmed stretch is (190, 0)–(200, 0). Turning back west: at (190, 0) the fix is on that stretch → holds, 570 − 240 + 25 = 355 against 330. At (150, 0): nothing at or after within 10 m (leg 2 is 20 m away); leg 1's (150, 0) is within 10 m and earlier → turned back → **395 against 370**. At (0, 0): the track's end at (0, 0) → **530 against 520** (track part, no allowance). Never short, 10 to 25 m long.
- **P:** fix (105, 49.9): nothing within 10 m; 49.9 m from the confirmed stretch, over 25 → today's rule: **515 against 465**. Never short.

**Never short?** To the self-join's standard, yes: if every fix is within 10 m of where the walker stands, a fix landing within 10 m of a stretch means they are on it or within 20 m of it, and the earliest such stretch at or after where they were last confirmed is never past them; a fix off by more than 10 m lands on nothing and either holds (up to 25 m) or falls back to today's rule. The hold is the one place it can read short under its own standard: a walker who turns back *and* walks 10 to 25 m off the line reads from the stretch they left, short by up to 25 m, until they are either back on the line (turned-back rule) or over 25 m from it (today's rule). Under the gate's 50 m standard it is short on **H**: a fix wrong by more than 10 m that lands within 10 m of a *later* part of the line, for as long as such fixes keep landing there, which on H is one fix. That is the exposure the owner is asked to accept, and it is the same exposure the track figure has had since the self-join ruling: `TrackSelfJoin.kt:13-20` says the 10 m is sound only while GPS error is within it, and the S22's outdoor figures are 3.79 m on 93% of fixes and up to 20 m on the rest (`LiveFixGate.kt:68-69`, one phone, three walks). A 30 m-wrong fix outdoors is not in that sample.

**Stale or jumping fix, checked.** Stale (no new fix): the figure stays; sundown degrades at STALE and withholds at LOST as now (`ReturnWalkingTime.kt:78, 95`), unchanged. Jumping fix within the gate: lands on nothing → holds or falls back, never short; lands on a later stretch → short for that fix, recovers at the next on-line fix (H above). Jumping fix beyond the gate: refused before any of this (`WayHomeWatch.kt:129`). A fix landing on the line but *ahead* of the walker along it by up to 10 m: the figure reads from that stretch's start, at most 10 m past them, covered on the trail part by the 25 m allowance and on the track parts by nothing: short by up to 10 m there, Sorrel's B-395-2 shape 1 again. Adding the 10 m to the track parts closes it; I would.

**What it costs the walker.** One number, falling as they walk home, 25 to 35 m long with a good fix; no climb beside their own wander. It climbs only when they are over 25 m off the line (then it is today's rule, and at 50 m the re-route), and it drops back when they return. The thing they could notice: after a wrong fix, one reading too low, then right; and under today's rule beside the line, the 60 to 85 m they already accepted.

**Size.** Medium-small, in the domain only. A new file for the figure rule (the confirmed-place search over stretches, point-to-segment distance, the three bands; about 120 to 160 lines, under the 400 limit), called from `WayHomeProgress.kt:139-142` (167 lines) in place of the one-line reach search; the confirmed place rides in `WayHomePlace`, which `WayHomeWatch` already keeps as `lastPlace` (`:88, 139`), so the watch needs little or no change; the HUD tick's own call with `previous = null` (`TrackRecordingRouteTicks.kt:106`) gets today's rule, as the fallback says. PR C untouched: it reads `remainingMeters` and nothing else. ## 8. Recommendation: B

B is the only candidate that keeps one figure, keeps it on the line drawn, does not climb on the ordinary forager pattern, and reads tighter than today everywhere else. Its never-short claim is to the standard the project already relies on for the track figure, stated in `TrackSelfJoin.kt`'s ruling and never contradicted by a measured outdoor fix on the one phone measured. Its exposure is one fix wrong by more than 10 m that lands on a later part of the line, which reads short until the next on-line fix. A shares that standard but with worse semantics (a number for a route other than the one drawn) and worse exposure (a false join persists for a hundred metres). The baseline is the only one never short at the gate's 50 m, and the owner should weigh that squarely: it is the one rule with no model behind it at all, and it costs a figure that climbs to the whole route beside the car on the walk most foragers take.

If the owner wants the gate's guarantee kept for sundown no matter what, D is the way to say so, and it should be said knowing that the alert then fires at the car.

**What B asks the owner to rule.** (1) That the figure may trust a fix to the self-join's 10 m, as the track figure does; the record so far says "never short for any fix the gate admits" (-1618, -1620), and B is not that. (2) The 25 m hold band (I propose the trail-join figure; the alternative is the 20 m hop-leave figure). (3) Whether "lands on a stretch" is inclusive at 10 m (then H reads short for one fix) or strict (then H falls to today's rule and reads 495). I would take strict. (4) Whether the 10 m line step is added on the track parts (B-395-2 shape 1); I would.

**Tests that would pin it**, for the dispatch: the S fixture end to end (figure never rises while fixes walk the trail west; 65 to 75 m at 20 m); H with the wrong fix then the recovering fix; T; P; a hold at 15 m off; a turn-back at 15 m off (reads from the stretch left, then recovers); today's rule governing until the first on-line fix. Fail-first; revert checks reading the build log first and restoring from a saved copy (CLAUDE.md).
