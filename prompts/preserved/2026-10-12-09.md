## What the owner saw

The owner tried PR #221's build on their phone. Verbatim: "When holding the phone in landscape while navigating, it does turn the map sideways as if it's still measuring portrait orientation. When using landscape in navigation mode, can the camera face the right way?"

Asked, they confirmed that "camera" means the map's view, not the photo camera.

The behaviour the owner wants: in landscape, while navigating, the heading-up map turns so the direction the person is facing points to the top of the screen, exactly as it does in portrait.

## Premises to check (unverified; the planner has not read this code)

1. **Where it goes wrong.** While navigating the map is heading-up (RECORD -942's survey: `NavigationHud.kt`, `northArrowDegrees`). The likely cause is that the device heading is computed in the phone's natural (portrait) frame, without accounting for the display rotation. Android's sensor-to-display remapping (`SensorManager.remapCoordinateSystem` with the display rotation, or adding the rotation's degrees to the azimuth) is the usual fix. **Confirm the actual cause in the code, with file and line, before changing anything.** It may lie elsewhere: the map camera's bearing, a GPS-course fallback, or the heading source.
2. **Whether main has it too.** PR #221 didn't touch the heading code. Say whether this bug exists at `a576c051`, the main before #221, from the code.
3. **What else reads the same heading.** The north arrow in the card, the heading text ("134° SE"), the compass strip when not navigating, the turn needle's frame, the walker's dot's direction cone, and anything else. Each must be right in landscape after the fix, at both rotations (90 and 270), and unchanged in portrait. A fix that turns the map but leaves a readout 90° off is not a fix.
4. **Reverse landscape (ROTATION_270)** is the case most likely to go wrong in the other direction. Check its sign specifically.
