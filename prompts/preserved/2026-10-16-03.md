## What to write

`docs/process/qa-philosophy-draft.md`. The owner reads it on a phone, and is a systems thinker, not a programmer: write about behaviour and consequences, not code names. Aim for about 1,500 words. Put anything longer in an appendix.

1. **What quality means for Forager.** A short statement of purpose. Forager is a field instrument used in the woods, often one-handed, offline, in poor light and with a low battery.
2. **The principles.** Each is one line, then a short explanation and the test an auditor applies. Mark each principle as one of two kinds:
   - **"The owner's words"**: quoted, with its source (a RECORD ID or a CLAUDE.md section);
   - **"Proposed by the auditor"**: new, with the reason and the prior art behind it.

   Never restate the owner's words as a numbered rule of your own.
3. **What "tested, debugged, and approved for public use" means for a promotion into main.** The evidence the auditor reads:
   - CI on pre-main;
   - device checks of a plain build, without dev tools, on the S22 Ultra and the S26 Ultra, and on the tablet when screens change;
   - known bugs;
   - accessibility;
   - copy.

   Say which items only a phone can show. CLAUDE.md records that Robolectric reports zero window insets, and that a semantic click doesn't test what a finger gets.
4. **How an audit runs and what it reports.** The verdict scale: pass, pass with items, fail. Each item says where it is, what the user would experience, and why it matters. The verdict advises; the owner decides. Urgent fixes for a live bug take the same gate at the front of the queue.
5. **What the auditor does not do.** It doesn't write app code, doesn't merge, and doesn't decide on the owner's behalf.
6. **Questions for the owner.** Every proposed principle and every choice the owner must make, listed with a recommendation each, and numbered so the owner can answer from a phone.
