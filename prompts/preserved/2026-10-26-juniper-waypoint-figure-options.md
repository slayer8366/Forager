## 0. The one-paragraph version

The waypoint figure is the way home's figure, reused as it is: the line's metres from the earliest point within 60 m of the fix anywhere on the line, plus 25 m when that point is on the trail (read, `WayHomeProgress.kt:148-152`; `AvailabilityWaypointRoute.kt:135`). That rule is never short for any fix the gate admits, and it costs 60 to 99 m of reading high along the whole route (computed). On the way home that margin sits against hundreds of metres and the owner accepted it (-1618, -1620, -1623). On a waypoint's final leg of 40 m it sits against 40 m, so the last number before "Arrived" is **115 m (377 ft) with 16 m left** on the S22 walk's shape (computed; Hazel read 412 ft at the end of the trail, the replica gives 410). Four ways to read it tighter were computed. **Dropping the 25 m** (O2) keeps the never-short guarantee whole and reads 25 m lower wherever the figure's point is on the trail: **90 m (295 ft)** before "Arrived" on that shape. **Reading the straight line once on the final leg** (O3) is exact only for the last metre or two before "Arrived" on a 40 m leg, drops by 100 m in one step on a 100 m leg, and reads short by the whole route on a trail that passes near its own end (−479 m). **Reading the fix's reported accuracy as the reach** (O4, the "candidate E" the owner deferred at -1623) reads **40 m (131 ft)** before "Arrived" with the S22's 4 m, never climbs beside a parallel leg 30 m away, and is short by the stretch of line it skips whenever the phone's position is wrong by more than twice what it claims: −87 m on the S22 shape, −478 m on the parallel legs. The way home's reasons for never-short (a safety input to sundown, the walk back in failing light) do not carry to the waypoint figure, which nothing but the HUD's large slot reads; what does carry is the owner's own standard and "Don't make me think". **I recommend O2 now** and holding O4 for the walk data the owner already asked for. ## 1. What is built, and why it reads high at a waypoint

**The line** (read, `FindWayToWaypointUseCase.kt:81-89`): a straight lead-in from the walker's fix at Navigate to the trail point nearest them, the engine's trail route, a straight final leg from the trail point nearest the waypoint to the waypoint. The two legs are `STRAIGHT`, the middle `TRAIL`. Hazel's walk: lead-in 40 m, trail about 350 m, final leg 40 m (read, her step 1).

**The figure** (read, `WayHomeProgress.kt:133-154`): at each fix, the earliest node of the whole line within 60 m of the fix; the figure is the line's metres from that node, plus 25 m if the node is on the trail part, plus the walker's distance back to the line's first point when they are past it (-1630). The 60 m is the gate's 50 m plus the line's 10 m node step (-1620); the 25 m is -1613's allowance, which `:202-209` describes as "now a margin on top" since Amendments 3 and 4, kept because "neither amendment removed it", and provisional. The needle's place is a different search ("not before the last place", `:139-143`); the figure deliberately ignores it since Sorrel's B-395-1 (-1617, -1618).

**Who reads the waypoint figure** (read): the HUD's large slot, through `WaypointRouteShown.hud` at `CompactMapPortraitNavigationCard.kt:124` and `CompactMapLandscapeNavigationCard.kt:169`, and nothing else: a search of `app/src/main` for `waypointRoute` finds no other reader; the step 6 report's §2.2 item 8 says a waypoint's distance "must not feed sundown" (read on `origin/waypoint-trails-report`); dispatch 2026-10-26-11 says "Sundown and Back by unchanged". Arrival is judged on the straight distance, not the figure (read, `ui/availability/NavigationReadout.kt:101, 112`; `domain/NavigationReadout.kt:67-71`: within the larger of 15 m and twice the reported accuracy). "Approaching" shows within 100 m straight (`:88`).

**Why it looks worse at a waypoint than on the way home** (computed, inferred): the margin is the same size, 60 to 99 m, in both places; the way home's last part is the walker's own track to the car, and a waypoint's is a straight leg, and both are usually short, so both show the full margin at the end. The difference the S22 walk made visible is that a waypoint's final leg leaves the trail's last node within 60 m of the whole leg when the leg is under 60 m, so the 25 m trail allowance stays on all the way to "Arrived". The owner saw the way home's version of the same thing and kept it (-1623); the question here is whether the waypoint's version is the state CLAUDE.md names as the worst, one the user can misread as broken: **377 ft, then "Arrived"**.

## 7. Why the way home's reasons for never-short do and do not carry

**The reasons, read.** `PathHome.kt:15-24`: a short figure is "the one direction that gets someone caught out after dark", and "Its error is never short, and that's the only property that matters in a safety input." The figure is a safety input because sundown reads it as the walk back (-1589 "app-wide"; `SundownWatch` through `AppContainer`, confirmed in the step 6 report and both rising-figure documents; not re-read by me at the file). -1618 and -1620 chose never short for "any fix the gate admits"; -1623 kept it beside a 630 m reading at the car.

**What does not carry** (read, inferred): nothing decides from the waypoint figure. No sundown or alert reads it (the one reader is the HUD slot, §1); arrival is the straight distance's and cannot be made early by a short figure or late by a long one; a waypoint is a find or a spot, not the car, and a return under way is paused and picks up again when the navigation ends (`WaypointNavigation.kt:19`, read), so the way home is never the thing being measured here. A short waypoint figure costs the walker a wrong expectation, hundreds of metres on U-shaped ground under Options 3 and 4 when the fix is wrong; it does not cost them the way home.

**What does carry** (inferred). (1) A waypoint the user made by hand where the car is would be their way home in intent and the waypoint figure in code; the designated origin is hidden from the map's bubbles (`AvailabilityPureFunctions.kt:70` keeps `designation == null`, read), but a hand-made one is an ordinary waypoint. (2) "Don't make me think": a figure that is short then long, or that drops by 100 m in one step, makes the user think; so does 377 ft then "Arrived". (3) The owner's own standard: one large slot, two rules for it, is a thing to choose knowingly, and the same reported-accuracy question would then be open for the way home with the walk data.
