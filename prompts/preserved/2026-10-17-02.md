## The question

For trail navigation in the app, does federal trail data help alongside OpenStreetMap's trails, and where?

**Sources:**
- the Forest Service's National Forest System Trails (CC BY 4.0);
- BLM trails (public domain);
- the USGS National Transportation Dataset's federal trail segments ("None", acknowledgment appreciated).

These were found for forecast part B; see forager-forecast branch t7-searches, `docs/audits/2026-10-17-t7-searches.md`, and Forager RECORD -1168.

## Method (read-only, sampled)

Pick 6 to 8 sample areas of about 10 x 10 km. Write down the choice and the reason before any data is read. Cover:
- a busy national forest near a city (for example the Mount Hood area);
- a wilderness (Eagle Cap, which is in Wallowa-Whitman, a forest that publishes little geometry);
- the Idaho interior;
- a BLM-dominated area;
- a coastal forest;
- one area outside national forests.

For each area:
1. **OSM:** query OpenStreetMap trail and path ways (footway, path, track, bridleway; and `highway=path` with `sac_scale` or trail tags) through a public query service. Small bounded queries only, with polite rate limits. Record each query and its time. Count the ways and sum their length.
2. **Federal:** query the federal trail layers' map services for the same box, as counts and lengths, with geometry only for the small sample. Don't download any national file.
3. **Overlap:** buffer each set by about 15 m. Report what share of each set's length lies near the other:
   - federal-only length is what federal data would add;
   - OSM-only length is what OSM has that federal data lacks.
4. **Usefulness for navigation:**
   - do the lines join up into a connected network (how many connected pieces, the largest piece's share of length)?
   - do they carry names, trail numbers, allowed uses, closures, difficulty?
   - when were they last updated, where stated?
5. **Disagreements:** where both have the same trail, how far apart are they typically? Sample 20 matched segments and give the median offset.

## Predictions

- **Outcome (planner):** OSM has more trail length in most areas, especially near cities. Federal data adds official names, numbers and allowed uses, and some backcountry trails OSM lacks. In Eagle Cap, federal geometry is nearly absent and OSM is the only usable source.
- **Mechanism:** state your sampling and matching method at the top of the report before reading any data.
