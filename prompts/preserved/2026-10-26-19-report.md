## Summary

1. **One place computes the walk-back time: `SundownWatch`.** It uses the walker's recorded track before Return, and the distance along the way home after Return when a way home along trails is shown and the walker is placed on it. Nothing else reads the walking time. **Back by does not use it**, and neither does any HUD figure except the sundown line's "start back by". The dispatch assumed Back by did.
2. **Before Return, the allowance can cover the whole path home.** The path is the walker's own stored points, and every one of them has a height.
3. **After Return on a way home along trails, it can cover none of the path today.** The trail part has no heights anywhere: the route files carry none, and the app's route type has no field for them. The two track parts lose their heights when the way home is built, and the sundown watch gets only a distance in metres. Owner choice **C1** decides what happens to the trail part.
4. **The pace already slows on the hills of the way out.** It is measured over the whole walk, hills included. So adding 2.2 s per metre on top counts part of that climb twice when the way back is the way out. On the 10-24 walk that is small. Owner choice **C2** decides whether to accept it.
5. **A phone with no barometer would over-count the climb 2 to 5 times.** Without the barometer the app smooths GPS heights, and on the 10-24 walk those read 106 to 168 m against a 70 m hill. Owner choice **C3**.
6. **On the 10-24 walk's shape, the figure changes by about two minutes today.** Standing at the far point, the walk back grows by about 2 min 10 s (recording A) and 2 min 16 s (recording B). After dispatch -18's fix it would be about 3 to 4.5 min. "Start back by", the heads-up and the leave-by alert all move earlier by the same amount.

## 1. Where the walk-back time is computed today

- **The single computation.** `domain/ReturnWalkingTime.kt:86-110`, `estimateAlong`, computes `walkingMillis = path.totalMeters / pace.speedMetersPerSecond` (`:107`). It has no climb, grade or height term. Two entry points share it:
  - `returnWalkingTime` (`:57-68`) uses the recorded track. It goes through `pathHome` (`domain/PathHome.kt:98-116`), which takes the shortest route home along the track joined to itself, from its most recent point back to its first (`joinedTrackHome`, `domain/TrackSelfJoin.kt:74-75`). It adds the hop to the walker's fix (in its band) and the leg to the origin waypoint (`PathHome.kt:160`).
  - `returnWalkingTimeAlong` (`ReturnWalkingTime.kt:77-83`) uses a `PathHome` handed in from the way home.
- **The pace.** `movingPace` (`domain/MovingPace.kt:98`) is measured over all of the track's points (`ReturnWalkingTime.kt:87`). It is the duration-weighted Doppler speed over moving intervals, or the differenced speed, or the 2 mph default (`MovingPace.kt:59-68`).
- **The one caller.** `SundownWatch.tick` (`domain/SundownWatch.kt:296-301`) calls one of the two entry points:
  - `returnWalkingTimeAlong` when `wayHomeFor(id)` gives a path;
  - otherwise `returnWalkingTime` over the track.

  `wayHomeFor` is wired at `AppContainer.kt:477`: the walker's place on the way-home line, as a `PathHome` (`domain/wayhome/WayHomeProgress.kt:101-102`, `trackMeters = remainingMeters`). It is non-null only while the walker is returning and has been placed (`domain/wayhome/WayHomeWatch.kt:136-140`). This is -1498 answer 4, "The route shown (Recommended)" (RECORD.md:25657). A grep of the main source set for `returnWalkingTime`, `returnWalkingTimeAlong`, `movingPace(` and `sundownLeaveByAt` finds no other production caller.
- **What reads the result:**
  - `sundownLeaveByAt` (`domain/SundownLine.kt:66-67`) computes turnaround minus walk back. Both the alerts (`DecideSundownAlertUseCase.kt:72`) and the line's "start back by" (`SundownWatch.kt:401-402`; `ui/availability/SundownLineText.kt:76-77`) use it.
  - The heads-up falls 30 min before that time (`DecideSundownAlertUseCase.kt:15`; `SundownWatch.kt:446-452`).
  - The notification text reads "about X" or "at least X" (`alert/AndroidAlertDelivery.kt:380-391`, strings `res/values/strings.xml:22-27`), with the duration rounded up to the minute (`AndroidAlertDelivery.kt:399-408`).
- **Back by does not read the walk-back time.** `BackByWatch`'s constructor takes no estimate, pace or path (`domain/BackByWatch.kt:62-78`). It alerts at a clock time the walker set. Juniper's grep found no climb term in `BackByWatch.kt`, `WayHomeWatch.kt`, `SundownLine.kt` or `DecideSundownAlertUseCase.kt`. That still holds: none of them computes a time from distance.
- **The HUD's distance home is metres, not time** (`domain/wayhome/WayHomeAhead.kt:39`, `routeMeters = place.remainingMeters`). The allowance does not touch it. The HUD shows the same sundown line as the strip (`SundownLine.kt:7-11`), so "start back by" moves there too.

## 2. Where the heights come from on that path

### Before Return, and after Return when the way home is the track

- Every stored point carries `altitude` (GPS) and, on a phone with a barometer, `pressureHpa`. Juniper's report §1 cites `data/local/TrackPointEntity.kt:31-44` and `service/TrackRecordingService.kt:220`. Pressure is attached through `pressureForPoint` (`domain/PressureProvider.kt:30-34`; caller `service/TrackRecordingService.kt:194` and following).
- `trackHeights` (`domain/TrackHeights.kt:18-21`) gives one height per point:
  - barometric when at least half the points carry a pressure (`domain/BarometricHeight.kt:43-100`);
  - smoothed GPS otherwise (`TrackHeights.kt:40-58`).
- The route home along the joined track is available as point indices: `joinedTrackRoute(...).route` (`TrackSelfJoin.kt:78`, `:145-160`, `:163-168`). `pathHome` drops the indices today, keeping only `meters` (`TrackSelfJoin.kt:74-75`, `PathHome.kt:102`). So a climb along the actual route home can be computed from heights the app already reads. It needs one new carry, the route's indices.
- **The hop and the origin leg have no heights.** The hop is a straight line to the live fix (`PathHome.kt:104`). The origin leg runs to the waypoint (`:106`). Both are counted flat.

### After Return, when a way home along trails is shown

- **The trail part has no heights at any level:**
  - The route files carry no elevation (`docs/plans/2026-10-21-trail-navigation-plan-update.md:58`; ruled "Flat first, size elevation in parallel", `:82`, RECORD -1498 item 2).
  - The app's route type is `TrailRoute(points: List<LatLng>, lengthMetres: Int)` (`domain/routing/TrailRouter.kt:41-45`).
  - The adapter maps the engine's nodes to latitude and longitude only (`routing/BRouterTrailRouter.kt:114`).
  - The vendored engine itself does carry a per-node elevation when its data has one (`third_party/brouter/src/main/java/btools/mapaccess/OsmNode.java:28`, `:72`; `Short.MIN_VALUE` when absent, `btools/router/FormatGpx.java:262`). **So Aspen's elevation build alone will not reach the walk-back time.** The adapter and `TrailRoute` also have to carry elevation.
- **The two track parts lose their heights too.** `FindWayHomeUseCase.trackPart` builds each one from stored points but keeps only `LatLng` (`domain/wayhome/FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:167-174`, `:173`). `WayHomePart` has no height field (`domain/wayhome/WayHome.kt:58`). `WayHomeLine` fills the parts in to 10 m nodes, still positions only (`WayHomeProgress.kt:26-70`).
- **What reaches `SundownWatch` is one number.** `toPathHome` sets `trackMeters = remainingMeters` and drops the hop and the origin (`WayHomeProgress.kt:101-102`). Even the track parts' climb could not be added without carrying heights (or stored-point indices) through `WayHomePart`, `WayHomeLine` and `WayHomePlace`.

### What the allowance can honestly cover today

| Situation | Path the walk-back time uses | Heights available | Allowance can cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before Return (the heads-up and leave-by mostly fire here) | the joined track home | every stored point's | **all of it**, except the hop and the origin leg (flat) |
| After Return, the way home is the track (`TrackOnly`, any note) | the same | the same | **all of it** |
| After Return, way home along trails, walker placed | track back + trail + track to start | track parts: yes, once carried through; trail: **none** | **the two track parts only**, after a change to carry their heights |
| After Return, along trails, walker off the line (`OffLine`) | falls back to the track (`SundownWatch.kt:296-297`) | the track's | all of it |

**What it would show for a way home that is mostly trail.** With the allowance on the track parts only, the trail part's climb goes uncounted, without a word. The figure would include the climb of the short track stretches at each end and none of the trail's. A climb on the trail would read exactly as today, while the figure looks like it now includes climbs. That is the silent partial coverage the dispatch says must go to the owner. It is choice C1.

There is one discontinuity either way. At Return the figure switches from the track (full climb) to the way home (partial or none). The walk-back time can drop by the whole allowance at the moment of Return, with no change in the ground ahead. The line's distance can already change at Return, but this drop would come from the allowance alone.

## 3. How climb would be measured on the path

- **Heights:** `trackHeights` as above.
  - Barometric: a median over 60 s each side, a 0.5 m/s step limit, a mean over 60 s each side, then an anchor to GPS (`BarometricHeight.kt:43-100`, constants `:131-158`).
  - Smoothed GPS: `TrackHeights.kt:40-58`.
- **Climb:** the journal's Climb and Descent bank changes through a 4 m hysteresis (`ComputeTrackStatisticsUseCase.kt:66-92`, `ElevationHysteresis.THRESHOLD_METERS = 4.0` at `:114-116`). A point with no height resets the reference (`:78-81`). The natural build reuses that banking over the heights of the route-home indices.
- **Direction does not matter.** The owner chose one figure, 2.2 s, for both climbing and descending (RECORD.md:28487). So the allowance is 2.2 s × (climb + descent along the route). The way home reverses the way out, so that total is the same either way it is walked. Banking a reversed sequence can differ by under one threshold (4 m) at the ends, about 9 s.
- **Dispatch -18 decides how much climb the allowance sees.** On the S26 walk, the scattered points without pressure break the barometric runs (`BarometricHeight.kt:40-41`, `:48-53`, `runsOf` at `:106-119`) and reset Climb (`ComputeTrackStatisticsUseCase.kt:78-81`). The allowance would see about 31 m where 53 m is the bridged figure, and 41 m where 72 m is. That is Juniper's §3, not re-run here. Built before -18 lands, it would add roughly 55 to 60 % of the intended amount on that phone. **Sequencing recommendation (a planner call, not the owner's):** build after -18 merges, on top of it.
- **Phones without a barometer, or a track under half covered by pressure:** smoothed GPS. On the 10-24 walk that reads 168/169 m (A) and 106/93 m (B) at 4 m hysteresis, against a 34.5 and 73.6 m barometric range (Juniper §3). That is 2 to 5 times the climb. At 2.2 s/m it would add about 12.4 min (A) and 7.3 min (B) over each whole recording, mostly from GPS wander. This is choice C3.
- **Cost (inferred, not measured).** `barometricHeights` scans the whole run for each point's window (`BarometricHeight.kt:52`, `:75`), so it grows with the square of the point count. Today it runs when a track's statistics are shown. The allowance would run it on every 15 s sundown tick (`service/TrackRecordingService.kt:559`) over the whole track, which is 1,135 points on the 10-24 outing. Probably milliseconds, but no measurement exists. A build should measure it or cache the heights per tick.

### How the never-short and "leans long" rulings interact

- **The never-short rulings are about distance.** `PathHome.kt:15-24` sets the most-recent-point rule. RECORD -1620 widens the figure's reach to 60 m (RECORD.md:27735-27740; `WayHomeProgress.kt:238`). RECORD -1623 keeps the rule plus 25 m on the trail (RECORD.md:27773; `WayHomeProgress.kt:226`).
- **The allowance adds time, not metres.** The extra metres those rulings add (up to 60 m of reach, plus 25 m on the trail) are counted flat, with no climb. To stay never short, the climb has to be counted from the same node the distance is: the earliest node within the reach (`WayHomeProgress.kt:152-156`). On the track home it is counted from the most recent stored point (`PathHome.kt:103`). Counting from the walker's true place would leave out climb they have not yet done.
- **"Leans long" (`MovingPace.kt:89-97`).** Doppler pace does not inflate the way the path length does, so the estimate already reads long. Juniper measured the way back reading 19 % (B) and 44 % (A) long on these returns (§3, out-and-back table). The allowance adds to that lean. It does not offset it.
- **"Never padded."** `ReturnWalkingTime.kt:15-22` and `MovingPace.kt:15-21` record the owner's earlier condition: the walk-back time is "never padded", and conservatism lives in the darkness margin. The allowance is a modelled term on the owner's ruling (-1687), not a pad. A build should still update both headers, and the darkness-margin note at `domain/SundownPreferencesRepository.kt:25-30`, so a later reader does not take it for one.
- **The descent figure.** The owner's 2.2 s for descending sits at the bottom of Juniper's interval for it: c = 3.19 s, 90 % 2.15 to 4.20 (§3). By that fit, descents lean short. This is recorded, not a question: the owner chose one figure knowing both.

## 5. Options where the owner must choose

### C1. The trail part of a way home has no heights. What does the walk-back time do there?

Sketch, after Return on a way home along trails:

```
start ●- - - track to start - - -●======== trail (no heights) ========●- - track back - -● you
       climb known (needs carrying)        climb unknown                climb known (needs carrying)
```

- **(a) Track parts only, and say so.** The trail part is counted flat and the figure keeps "about". The figure is short of what it would be with the trail's climb, with nothing on screen to say so. It never reads shorter than today. It needs the track parts' heights carried through the way home (`FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:173`, `WayHome.kt:58`, `WayHomeProgress.kt:26-70`, `:101-102`), files owned by steps 7 and 8.
- **(b) Use the walker's own track as a stand-in for the trail part.** Count the climb of the walker's own track between the same two trail points: the walker walked between them, the trail lies within 1.5 times that length (`WayHome.kt:138`; `FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:98`), and its net rise is the same. It can still be short where the trail crosses a rise the walker's track went round. The label stays "about".
- **(c) Say "at least" while any part lacks heights.** Add a degrade reason for a path with an unheighted part, so the alerts say "The walk back is at least 31 min … at the latest" (strings `:23`, `:26`). This is honest about the gap and uses wording that already exists. It shows on nearly every trail way home until elevation lands, so walkers would see "at least" often.
- **(d) Keep the track-based walk-back time, with its full climb, after Return too.** This reverses -1498 answer 4, "The route shown" (RECORD.md:25657), so it is listed only for completeness.
- **(e) Track parts now; the trail part when elevation lands.** Same as (a), with a follow-on dispatch for the adapter (`BRouterTrailRouter.kt:114`, `TrailRouter.kt:41-45`) once Aspen's elevation data is in the route files. -1628 puts elevation routing inside this release (RECORD.md:27823 and following).

**Recommendation: (b) now, with (e) as the follow-on.** It is the only option that counts some trail climb without inventing a figure. It does not show "at least" on every trail way home. And it drops the Return discontinuity to the difference between trail and track.

### C2. The measured pace already slows on the walk's own climbs

The pace is measured over the walk so far, hills included (`ReturnWalkingTime.kt:87`; `MovingPace.kt:98`). On a way back that retraces the way out, adding 2.2 s per metre counts that climb a second time, in the share the pace already carries.

- **(a) Add it as ruled.** It leans long, consistent with the safety direction. On the 10-24 walk the share is within noise: the app's Doppler pace for B is 0.930 m/s, against Juniper's fitted flat pace of 0.922 m/s (§3).
- **(b) Take the climb out of the pace first, then add the way back's climb.** This uses Juniper's model as fitted: seconds = a·metres + b·climb + c·descent. The walk back becomes shorter than in (a). It reads shorter than today wherever the way out was hillier than the way back.

**Recommendation: (a).** It is the owner's ruling as given. The double count is small on the only walk measured and errs long. A build should record it in `ReturnWalkingTime.kt`'s header so nobody "corrects" one half without the other.

### C3. Phones without a barometer (smoothed GPS heights)

- **(a) Apply the allowance anyway.** It reads long, by about 7 to 12 min over the 10-24 recordings, mostly from GPS wander. A flat walk can also gain minutes.
- **(b) No allowance without a barometer.** Same as today on those phones, short on real hills, and stated in code and docs.
- **(c) Apply it with a coarser hysteresis for GPS (10 m).** Per Juniper's §3 this still reads about 1 to 2 times the climb. It adds a new figure, which needs its own reason.

**Recommendation: (b).** On the only data there is, the GPS climb is mostly noise, and 2.2 s per metre of noise moves the sundown alerts by minutes for nothing. Both of the owner's phones have a barometer. The S26 reads barometric (Juniper §2), and the S22's walks carry pressure (`BarometricHeight.kt:27-30`).

## 6. Mechanism prediction for Phase 2 (if the owner says go)

These are coder-level calls, listed for the planner to accept or reject:

- `pathHome` keeps the route's indices (`joinedTrackRoute`, already computed). A new pure function gives climb + descent along those indices from `trackHeights`, banked as `ComputeTrackStatisticsUseCase` banks.
- `PathHome` gets a nullable climb-and-descent field. `null` means not measured, never zero.
- `estimateAlong` adds 2.2 s per metre to `walkingMillis` (`ReturnWalkingTime.kt:107`). `Estimate` carries the climb and the allowance so a test checks the arithmetic against its inputs, as the class asks (`:114-118`).
- The coefficient is one named constant whose doc quotes -1687.
- The way home's parts carry heights or indices per C1.
- Tests go first, over stubs:
  - an out-and-back fixture with a known climb;
  - a track with a mid-walk pressure gap (after -18);
  - a way home with a trail part, per C1;
  - an end-to-end check through `SundownWatch.tick`'s line and alert time, not a hand call to `estimateAlong`.
- The revert check is one edit: remove the allowance term.

Files this would touch beyond dispatch -18's two:
- `ReturnWalkingTime.kt`, `PathHome.kt`, `TrackSelfJoin.kt` (an accessor only);
- `SundownWatch.kt` (C1 wiring);
- for C1 (a), (b) or (e): `FindWayHomeUseCase.kt`, `WayHome.kt`, `WayHomeProgress.kt`, which steps 7 and 8 also work in. The planner sequences these.

### Premises that were wrong

- **The dispatch lists Back by among the figures that change.** Back by is a clock time the walker sets and reads no walk-back time (`BackByWatch.kt:62-78`), so it does not change.
- **"Where the walk-back time is computed today (SundownWatch, BackByWatch, the way-home figure)".** Only `SundownWatch` computes it. The way-home figure is a distance handed in (`AppContainer.kt:477`).
- **"The walker's own track carries altitude and pressure".** True of the stored track. But the way home's track parts, as built, drop the heights (`FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:173`), so after Return on trails even the track parts have none today.
- **"The trail route from the engine carries no elevation … waits for Aspen's elevation build".** Partly. The route files carry none today, but the vendored engine has a per-node elevation field, and it is the app's adapter that drops it (`BRouterTrailRouter.kt:114`). Aspen's build alone will not bring it through. The adapter and `TrailRoute` must change too.
- **The one figure "for both".** 2.2 s matches Juniper's fit for climbing. For descending the fit was 3.19 s, so 2.2 sits near the bottom of its interval. This is noted, not contested.
