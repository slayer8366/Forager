## 1. Where the drawn trail geometry comes from (question 1)

The path from the data to the pixels, with nothing in it that simplifies:

1. **The Pi's build** (`pi-build` branch, `map-build/bin/build-orwa.sh:227-236`, the same three steps in `build-usca.sh`):
   BRouter 1.7.10's `OsmFastCutter`, `PosUnifier`, `WayLinker` on a Geofabrik extract. The USCA build first runs
   `osmium tags-filter w/highway w/railway w/waterway w/route r/route r/type=restriction` (`pins.env`, `USCA_ROUTING_FILTER`;
   `build-usca.sh:724`); osmium keeps the nodes the matched ways reference unless `--omit-referenced` is given, and it is not.
2. **The square's geometry.** In `WayLinker.nextWay` (brouter-map-creator at the pinned commit `4d2639af`, `WayLinker.java:366-387`)
   every node of a way that is in `nodesMap` becomes a link; a node that is *not* found is skipped and the link runs from
   the last found node to the next (`:368-374`), a straight chord. A node with exactly two links and only one way is a
   transfer node (`OsmNodeP.java:286-288`); when the square is written, each link's transfer nodes go through a
   Douglas-Peucker filter before they are stored (`OsmNodeP.java:222-233`, `DPFilter.java:13`, `:64`). **Its tolerance is
   0.4 m** (`dp_sql_threshold = 0.4 * 0.4`, squared metres): a node more than 0.4 m off the chord survives. It cannot drop a
   bend of metres.
3. **The route cutter (#375)** copies cells byte for byte (`server/pmtiles-worker/src/rd5/cut.ts:5-9`, `:76-87`); it changes
   no geometry.
4. **BRouter in the app** reads a link's geometry once, shared by its forward and reverse links
   (`third_party/brouter/.../mapaccess/OsmNode.java:164-189`; `OsmLink.java:15-18`). Its search runs two coarse passes and
   then a final "re-tracking" pass with `guideTrack` set (`RoutingEngine.java:1796-1801`); only that pass records transfer
   nodes (`OsmPath.java:123`, `recordTransferNodes = detailMode`; `:359-360`), and the returned track is that pass's. Its
   length is the sum over the recorded elements (`RoutingEngine.java:2269-2311`), so **the engine's `track.distance` and its
   `track.nodes` describe the same polyline**: if the data holds a bend, both have it; if not, neither does.
5. **The app** maps `track.nodes` straight to `TrailRoute.points` and `track.distance` to `lengthMetres`
   (`BRouterTrailRouter.kt:113-117`); `FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:87-92` puts the leave and entry track points at either end with
   straight joints of at most 25 m. `WayHomeLine` (`WayHomeProgress.kt:30-57`) fills every stretch over
   `WAY_HOME_LINE_STEP_METERS` (10 m) with nodes *on the straight line between the engine's points* (`:52`), and scales each
   part's along-metres so the line's total is the engine's figure plus joints plus the track parts (`:38`, `:53`): the HUD's
   516 m is `WayHome.totalMeters`, which is the engine's length, not a length re-measured from the drawn points.
6. **The map** draws the parts as GeoJSON line layers (`RouteHomeLayers.kt:144-159`, `:184-202`); MapLibre's default
   `GeoJsonSource` tolerance (0.375 px) simplifies for rendering only and touches no figure.

So the only places a bend can be lost are the data the Pi's square holds (step 2, with step 1's extract) and the engine's
own matching of the two ends to that data. Nothing after the engine's output, and nothing in the cutter, can remove one.

## 2. What the numbers say (Hazel's data, re-derived)

- Hazel's model of the line on OpenStreetMap geometry gives 542.6 m; mine, written independently from her stored points
  and her fetch, gives 542.6 m too (entry at c 250.9, 19.2 m off; leave at c 576.3, 13.9 m off; trail part 325.4 m on OSM
  plus 33.1 m of joints; track back 163.4 m; track to start 20.7 m). The app read 516 m. So the engine's trail route between
  those two feet is about 299 m against 325.4 m on OSM: **26.6 m, 8 %, short**.
- **The straight chord between the two feet is 304.6 m.** The engine's 299 m is at, or just under, the chord: the engine's
  trail part has *no* bow at all, or a joint arithmetic I have not accounted for. The OSM trail between the feet bows up to
  45.8 m off that chord (at c 359). This is the key finding of the draft: it is not one bend at the far end that is cut, it
  is the whole trail part drawn nearly straight.
- **OSM's geometry for this trail has not changed since 2018-11-26** (way version 13; every one of its 242 nodes last
  touched 2018-11-26, read from the OSM API's full way on 2026-10-25). Hazel's fetch carries `timestamp_osm_base`
  2026-07-28 and the Pi's extract is from October; the gap does not matter, the trail was identical in both. Data vintage
  is ruled out.
- **No other way the engine could have used.** The OSM API's current map of a 373 m by 693 m box around the trail part
  holds 15 ways: Hares Canyon Trail, four paths 150 m or more away, the Banks-Vernonia State Trail (`highway=cycleway`,
  outside Hazel's fetch, which took only `path`, `footway` and `track`) 148 to 350 m away with its only junction at the
  trail's c 0, five streams and a valley. The trail has no junction between c 0 and c 869 (shared nodes with other ways:
  Banks-Vernonia and the connector at c 0, Brooke Creek Trail at c 869), so **the whole 869 m is one BRouter link**, and both
  ends of the route lie inside it.
- **The engine's output on the Harriman fixture is dense**: `inside-long.geojson` (6,713 m by the engine) has 306 points,
  median spacing 18.8 m, the longest 88.5 m. So the pipeline as run for the fixture keeps shape nodes. Whether the Pi's
  square does for this link is the open question (section 5).

## 3. Can the figure read short, and by how much (question 3)

In progress; the settled part:

- The figure is the engine's length plus joints plus track parts (section 1, item 5). Where the engine's line lacks a
  bend that the walker walks, the figure is short of the walked distance by the bend's extra length, for as long as the
  walker is on the bent stretch and the figure's node is behind it. On this walk that was 27 m for the first 37 m after
  Return (Hazel's table, section 2a), and the 25 m allowance (-1618) did not cover it: 516 against 542.6 on the drawn
  line's own geometry, 553 walked.
- The worst case is bounded by how much longer a trail is than the chord between the engine's points: here 8 % of the
  trail part. [To be completed: the bound per link, and whether the reach and allowance cover it.]
