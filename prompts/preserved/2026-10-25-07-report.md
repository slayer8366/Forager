# Why the routed trail reads short: not a bend, the line's start (Sorrel, dispatch 2026-10-25-07)

Read-only, on Fable (claude-fable-5-1). No app code, no Gradle, no phone. Code read on pre-main `f5a0cfafc` (the
figure-check build was `fb692fb57`, one merge earlier; `git diff fb692fb57 f5a0cfafc --stat` lists only
`MapForecastCells.kt`, its new stale-style test and a style fixture, none of them cited here). Hazel's data read in
`~/.cache/s22-figure-check/`; nothing from it is copied here and no position appears in this report. Scratch under
`~/.cache/sorrel-bends/`. BRouter's map-creator sources (not vendored) were read at the Pi's pinned commit `4d2639af`
(`pins.env`, `BROUTER_COMMIT`), fetched from GitHub into that scratch directory.

## Summary

- **No step drops a bend.** The whole path from the Pi's square to the pixels was read (section 1): the only
  simplification anywhere is a 0.4 m Douglas-Peucker in the square's writer, the cutter copies cells byte for byte,
  BRouter's final pass records every shape node and sums its length over the same nodes it returns, and the app and the
  map add and remove nothing. A bend of metres cannot be lost on this path.
- **The drawn trail part is the trail's length.** Measured on Hazel's own screenshot, georeferenced six independent
  ways that agree to 2.5 %: the drawn line between the two trail feet is 320 to 326 m against 325.4 m on OpenStreetMap,
  and it follows the trail within 3 m over the first 190 m. Over its last 60 m it runs 7 to 16 m to one side of OSM's
  line, which is what Hazel saw as a cut bend; that stretch saves at most 5 m, not 27 (section 2).
- **The 27 m is the line's start, not its trail part.** The way home is found on the track as it stands in the
  database, and the service writes kept points in batches, every 30 s or 20 points, with no flush on Return. Hazel's
  Return came 418 s after Record on both recordings, so the last flush fell 28 s before it and the three points kept at
  26, 21 and 16 s before Return (18.7 m of walking) were still in the buffer. The line started 18.7 m behind the walker,
  the figure is that line's total, and both runs read the same 516 m because both had the same timing. The dots of the
  drawn track-back, a ruler laid from the line's first node, allow exactly that start and no other but the walker's own
  (section 3). Hazel's re-route finding F-1 and the earlier way-home check's "dotted run starts about 20 m short of the
  dot" are the same mechanism.
- **How short it can read:** up to one flush interval of walking, 30 s, about 42 m at 1.4 m/s, before Return or before
  each re-route; it stays short by that amount until the walker is one node step past the line's first node. The 25 m
  allowance does not apply (the figure's node is on the track part) and the 60 m reach anchors the figure to that stale
  first node rather than correcting it (section 4).
- **The fix sits at the step where the way home reads the track** (`WayHomeWatch.work`, and the same read at a
  re-route): the line should start at the walker's newest kept point, not the newest written one. Section 5.

## 1. Where the drawn trail geometry comes from (question 1), with what each step can and cannot drop

1. **The Pi's build** (`pi-build`, `map-build/bin/build-orwa.sh:227-236`; the same three steps in `build-usca.sh`):
   BRouter 1.7.10's `OsmFastCutter`, `PosUnifier`, `WayLinker` on Geofabrik extracts. The USCA build first runs
   `osmium tags-filter w/highway w/railway w/waterway w/route r/route r/type=restriction` (`pins.env`,
   `USCA_ROUTING_FILTER`; `build-usca.sh:724`); osmium keeps the nodes a matched way references unless
   `--omit-referenced` is given, and it is not.
2. **The square's geometry.** `WayLinker.nextWay` (`WayLinker.java:366-387` at `4d2639af`) links every node of a way that
   is in its node map; a node that is *not* found is skipped and the link runs straight from the last found node to the
   next (`:368-374`). A node with two links and one way is a transfer node (`OsmNodeP.java:286-288`); when a link is
   written its transfer nodes pass a Douglas-Peucker filter (`OsmNodeP.java:222-233`; `DPFilter.java:13`, `:64`) whose
   tolerance is **0.4 m** (`dp_sql_threshold = 0.4 * 0.4`, squared metres). `NodeFilter` keeps every node any way
   references (`NodeFilter.java:50-54`, `:71-74`); `PosUnifier` moves a duplicate position by whole micro-degrees
   (`PosUnifier.java:172-190`). Nothing here can drop a bend of metres; a missing node could, and section 2 shows the
   square's geometry is not missing one on this link.
3. **The route cutter (#375)** copies cells byte for byte and rewrites only offsets, indexes and checksums
   (`server/pmtiles-worker/src/rd5/cut.ts:5-9`, `:76-87`).
4. **BRouter in the app.** A link's geometry is read once and shared by its forward and reverse links
   (`third_party/brouter/.../mapaccess/OsmNode.java:164-189`; `OsmLink.java:15-18`). The search runs two coarse passes and
   a final "re-tracking" pass with `guideTrack` set (`RoutingEngine.java:1793-1801`); only that pass records transfer
   nodes (`OsmPath.java:123`, `recordTransferNodes = detailMode`; `:359-360`), the returned track is that pass's, and
   its distance is summed over the elements it returns (`RoutingEngine.java:2269-2311`), then recomputed over every node
   of the final track (`recalcTrack`, `:1482-1526`, called at `:1038`), each leg rounded to the metre
   (`OsmPathElement.java:77-79`, `Math.round`). **The engine's length and its node list describe one polyline.** The
   Harriman fixture shows the pipeline as run keeps shape nodes: `brouter-cli/inside-long.geojson` has 306 points over
   6,713 m, median spacing 18.8 m, and its coordinates sum to 6,705 m against the engine's 6,713.
5. **The app** maps `track.nodes` to `TrailRoute.points` and `track.distance` to `lengthMetres`
   (`BRouterTrailRouter.kt:113-117`); `FindWayHomeUseCase.kt:87-92` puts the leave and entry track points at either end
   with straight joints of at most 25 m each. `WayHomeLine` (`WayHomeProgress.kt:30-57`) fills every stretch over
   `WAY_HOME_LINE_STEP_METERS` (10 m) with nodes *on the straight line between the engine's points* (`:52`) and scales
   each part's along-metres so the line's total is `WayHome.totalMeters` exactly (`:38`, `:53`): the figure at Return is
   the engine's length plus the joints plus the two track parts, not a length re-measured from the drawn points. The
   track parts are plain polylines on this walk: `joinedTrackRoute` joins only points within 10 m
   (`TrackSelfJoin.kt:104-110`), and Hazel's stored points are 6.2 m apart, so no non-consecutive pair joins.
6. **The map** draws the parts as GeoJSON line layers (`RouteHomeLayers.kt:144-159`, `:184-202`); MapLibre's default
   source tolerance simplifies for rendering only and touches no figure.

## 2. The drawn line is not shorter than the trail (question 2, first half)

**What was modelled.** From Hazel's stored points and her Overpass fetch, independently of her scripts: the scan finds
the entry at c 250.9 (19.2 m off the trail) and the leave at c 576.3 (13.9 m off); the trail part on OSM is 325.4 m
plus 33.1 m of joints, the track back 163.4 m, the track to the start 20.7 m: **542.6 m**, Hazel's figure to the
decimetre. Against 516 m read, the deficit is 26.6 m. The straight chord between the two feet is 304.6 m; no chord from
the leave foot back along the trail saves more than 5 m until c 336, and the only stretch where OSM bows away from
such a chord by more than 15 m is c 294 to 403, 170 m from the leave end. There is no single bend whose loss is 27 m.

**What could not have changed the data.** OSM's geometry for this trail is unchanged since 2018-11-26: way version
13, and every one of its 242 nodes last touched that day (read from the OSM API's full way on 2026-10-25). Hazel's fetch
carries `timestamp_osm_base` 2026-07-28 and the Pi's extracts are from October; the trail was identical in both. The
OSM API's current map of a box around the trail part holds no way the engine could have taken instead: four paths 150 m
or more away, the Banks-Vernonia State Trail (`highway=cycleway`, outside Hazel's fetch, which took only `path`,
`footway` and `track`) 148 to 350 m away with its only junction at the trail's c 0, five streams and a valley. The
trail has no junction between c 0 and c 869 (shared nodes: Banks-Vernonia and a connector at c 0, Brooke Creek Trail
at c 869), so **the whole stretch is one BRouter link**, with both route ends inside it.

**What the screenshot measures.** Hazel's full-resolution screenshot 10 (recording 2, the same 516 m) was
georeferenced by a four-parameter similarity fit: the dotted track-back against the stored points, the solid line
against OSM over c 251-440 only, the stretch Hazel's replica vouches for. Residual 3.5 m rms; the walker's dot lands
0.3 m from the last stored point, which the fit did not use. Scale: 2.230 px/m. Because the solid-line term could pull
the scale, it was re-derived five more ways that use no OSM at all: the dotted L alone with the dot pinned (2.237, a
clear minimum: 3.7 m rms against 4.4 at 2.1 and 4.8 at 2.4), the dot to the dotted corner found by intersecting the two
dotted legs (2.246), the dot to the Start marker's pin tip, pin head and crosshair centre against the stored start's
217.5 m (2.270, 2.231, 2.285). A base-map stream fit was tried and discarded (24 m rms; the base map generalises
streams at this zoom). **All six agree within 2.5 %, 2.23 to 2.29 px/m.** Traced column by column through the same
transform, the drawn solid line between the two feet is 728 px and the OSM trail 727 px: at 2.23 px/m both are 326 m;
at the top of the range the drawn line is 2.4 % shorter, 8 m. For it to be 299 m, the engine's length implied by
516, the scale would have to be 2.43, where every estimate is plainly worse. Offsets of the drawn line from OSM, by
fifths of its run: −1.7, +2.9, −1.9, −7.1, −15.6 m medians. So the drawn line follows the trail within 3 m over its
first three fifths and runs 7 to 16 m to one side over the last 60 m (c 500-580), where OSM's trail swings 28 m
sideways in its last 50 m: that is the cut bend of the dispatch, it is real, and the chord across it saves 5 m.

So the trail part accounts for at most 8 m of the 27, and probably none. The dispatch's premise, that the figure reads
short because the drawn trail cuts a bend, is not what the data show.

## 3. Which step: the line starts one write batch behind the walker (question 2, second half)

- The way home at Return is found on `trackRepository.getById` (`AppContainer.kt:441`; `WayHomeWatch.kt:204-213`), the
  track as written. The service keeps accepted points in a buffer and writes them when 20 have gathered or 30 s have
  passed (`TrackRecordingService.kt:245-249`, `:260-264`, `:546-547`); the flush is private to the service and is called
  only on those two conditions and at stop (`:249`, `:263`, `:389`, `:404-414`). Nothing on the Return path flushes.
  The return watch, by contrast, is handed each kept point directly (`:244`), so it never sees this lag.
- **Hazel's timing.** Record at 05:38:37, Return at 05:45:35: 418 s; recording 2 the same, 05:55:50 to 06:02:48. The
  timer's flushes fall every 30 s from the collector's start, the last at about 390 s, 28 s before Return. The stored
  points (read from the database afterwards) are 5 s apart; the last three before Return are at 26, 21 and 16 s before
  it (the walker then held still, and the sampler stored nothing more). Those three were in the buffer when the way home
  read the track; the line started at the point 18.7 m behind the walker. A count flush cannot have intervened: six
  points gather per 30 s, never twenty.
- **The dots confirm it, and rule the alternatives out.** The track-back is drawn as a dash pattern laid from the line's
  first node, one width on and two off (`RouteHomeLayers.kt:104`). Measured along the parallel leg, the dots sit 42 to
  43 px apart, 12 dp at the S22 Ultra's 3.5 px/dp, exactly the pattern, so their positions are a ruler from the line's
  start. Their phase against the walker's dot centre admits a start at the walker or 18.7 m behind it (three stored
  points are one dot period, so the two cannot be told apart by phase) and excludes 25, 31, 38 and 45 m. With the
  timer arithmetic, that leaves 18.7 m.
- **The arithmetic closes.** 542.6 − 18.7 = 523.9 m predicted against 516 read; the 8 m remaining is inside the
  model's slack (the joints are modelled to OSM, the engine's actual start lies about 15 m beyond the leave foot on the
  data's own line, and the trail part may be up to 8 m shorter at the top of the scale range). Both recordings read 516
  because both had the same 418 s and so the same three points in the buffer.
- **The same mechanism elsewhere in Hazel's data.** Her F-1: after each re-route the new line starts at the newest
  *stored* point and the figure does not grow as the walker walks on. And the S22 way-home check's "the dotted run
  started about 20 m short of the walker's dot". Both are this.
- The swap's track-only figure, 508 m against a 546 m stored track, is not explained by this and is left open (section
  7).

## 4. Can the figure read short, and by how much (question 3)

Yes, from this and independently of any bend. The figure is the stale line's total; at Return the walker is up to one
batch past its first node, the figure's node is that first node (within the 60 m reach, `WayHomeProgress.kt:141`), and
no allowance is added because that node is on the track part (`:142-144`). So the figure reads short of the walked
distance by the lag, which is **up to 30 s of walking: about 42 m at 1.4 m/s, 37 m at Hazel's 1.24 m/s**, and it stays
short by that until the walker is a node step past the first node; after that the earliest node within 60 m moves and
the figure is long, as Hazel's table shows (short by 37 to 27 m for the first 37 m, then 0 to 60 m long). The same
happens at every re-route, where the lag adds to the stray distance Hazel measured. The 25 m allowance (-1618) does not
cover it, because it is applied only when the figure's node is on the trail part; the 60 m reach (-1620) does not
correct it, because it is what anchors the figure to the stale first node in the first place. The 20-point cap never
bites at walking pace (six points per 30 s).

## 5. What would fix it, and at which step (question 4, not designed)

At the step where the way home reads the track, `WayHomeWatch.work` at Return and the same read at a re-route: start
the line from the walker's newest kept point rather than the newest written one. The return watch already receives
each kept point as it is accepted (`TrackRecordingService.kt:244`), which is the precedent. Two shapes the owner could
choose between, not priced here: the watch is handed the service's pending points, or the read waits for a flush. A
third, smaller, is Hazel's own F-1 question: count the walker's hop to the first node into the figure when that node is
the figure's node. Nothing on the Pi's side, in the cutter or in the engine needs to change for this.

read from the solid line's hook in the screenshot, not from the engine.

**Could not determine.** The content of the Pi's square for this link, beyond what the drawn line implies (section 7).
The swap's 508 m track figure. What the two short purple bars crossing the trail in the screenshot are (kept-track
style; not in Hazel's fetch or the OSM box; they do not touch the finding).

**Premises that were wrong.** The dispatch's: that the figure reads short because the drawn trail cuts a bend. The
drawn line does run to one side of OSM over its last 60 m, and that is not where the 27 m went.

**Decided beyond scope.** None. No code, no Gradle, no phone.
