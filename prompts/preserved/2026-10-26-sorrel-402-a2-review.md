## 1. The rule and the figure (the owner's "Coder proposes, Sorrel checks")

### 1.1 What is built

`isHeadingBackAlong(line, place, here)` (`WayHomeOnCourse.kt:98-102`): false when `metersPastLineStart(line, here) > 0` (#398's test, `WayHomeProgress.kt:164-174`); otherwise true when the place's distance along the line is above 0 (`line.alongMeters[place.index] > 0`), or when `metersAheadOfLineStart(line, here) >= WAY_HOME_LINE_STEP_METERS` (10 m; `WayHomeProgress.kt:181-191`, new, the same first-stretch projection as `metersPastLineStart` without the sign test). `ReturnWatch.onFix` (`ReturnWatch.kt:279-281`) calls it once per reading through `dottedPartNow` (`:371-375`) and passes the dotted part as one more also-on line only when it is true; the trail parts are kept as #399 has them (`takeTrailShown`, `:362-369`). The dotted part of an earlier line is no longer kept (`:165-166`). The track-only way home still maps to `null` (`shownTrailOf`, `WayHomeOnCourse.kt:35-40`, `line == null`). Matches -1640's three answers and Amendment 2's text.

### 1.2 Is 10 m defensible as the line's own point spacing?

Yes, with two things said about it.

- It is the node step (`WAY_HOME_LINE_STEP_METERS`, `WayHomeProgress.kt:224`), and the step is the longest stretch between two nodes after filling; the first stretch the projection runs along is the newest stored stretch, which in `HIGH_ACCURACY` is at least 5 m and 5 s (`TrackRecordingMode.kt:27`, `LocationSampler.kt:40-47`), so "10 m ahead" is at or beyond the second node for a walker at pace. The 0.5 m epsilon (`WayHomeProgress.kt:194`) is safe under that spacing.
- **What the figure buys, measured.** Standing at the far end of a stray (52 m from the trail) for 60 s with Gaussian GPS jitter of sigma metres on every fix, 200 trials each, trials in which the alert came: with 10 m, 200/200 at sigma 1, 3 and 5, 192/200 at 8, 151/200 at 10, 50/200 at 15. With 0 m (the coder's rejected "any distance ahead"): 200, 130, 34, 17, 10, 6. So the coder's reason for rejecting 0 m is right and large: at 3 m jitter, 0 m misses the standing alert in a third of trials, 10 m in none. With 15 m the numbers are identical to 10 m, so above 10 m the figure stops mattering here and the misses at sigma 8 and up are the latch of 1.4, not the threshold.
- **What it costs, measured.** Turning back right after the re-route (section 4 of the replica): with no store lag, a walker who turns 3 m past the re-route point gets one alert in their first 10 m back under 10 m and none under 0 m; at 6 m past, both alert (the 15 s hold is simply up: 40 m to 56 m out is 11.7 s, 6 m back is 4.3 s). So the 10 m costs an alert against 0 m in a window of about 3 m, 2 s. With the store lag (flush every 20 kept or 30 s, `TrackRecordingService.kt:546-547`) both figures alert at 3 m past, because the line's start is then 8 m behind the re-route point and the walker must come back past it first. The coder's two named costs (an alert in the first 10 m back after overshooting; the lag added to the 10 m until -10 lands) are the right two, and the lag is the larger until 2026-10-25-10 lands.
- The first stretch's direction is noisy, as the coder says ("the first stretch gives the direction"). With 2 m noise on stored points the dotted part starts counting a median 10.7 m back from the line's start, p90 12 m, max 22 m over 100 trials; with 4 m noise, median 10.9, p90 15.3, max 27.6. Half (a) caps this near 25 m (the place leaves node 0 once the fix is over 25 m from it and within 25 m of a later node, `WayHomeProgress.kt:134`). Acceptable; recorded so a device walk that counts 15 m back is not filed as a defect.

The figure is not derived from GPS statistics and the coder does not say it is; it is the smallest figure the line itself carries, and the replica says it is also enough against ordinary jitter. Defensible.

### 1.3 The three ruled behaviours

Read from the code and run in the replica with the production sampler (5 s / 5 m) and the store lag, instant and delayed (3 s, 8 s) route jobs:

- **A steady stray alerts exactly once per stray.** Out to 130 m from the trail past two re-routes: 1 alert, generation 3, with and without lag. Each re-route's line starts at the newest stored point, which is at or behind the walker, so a walker going on is past the start (`metersPastLineStart > 0`) and the dotted part never counts; the alert comes 15 s after 40 m off, as before #402, and `armed` stays false for the rest of that stray (`OffTrackJudge.kt:100-104`). This is 402-1 closed.
- **Following the line home never alerts.** Out to 200 m (four re-routes) and back to the trail: 1 alert (the one on the way out), `isOffTrack` false at the end, for job delays 0, 3 and 8 s. The dotted part counts from 10 m past the line's start, and a walker who was out that far has long since been alerted and is re-armed only by 10 s on course, so no second alert can come on the way in. The exception is the ~3 m, 2 s window of 1.2, which is the coder's named cost and the lag's until -10.
- **A pause counts.** Out to 110 m, back to 80 m, stand 60 s: 1 alert, `isOffTrack` false at the end, as the test asserts (no lag). With the lag the same walk ends `isOffTrack` **true** with still 1 alert: the line's start is 26 m behind the turn, so standing 30 m back along the stray is 4 m ahead of the start, under the 10 m, and the place is still at node 0. No alert comes of it (the walker was alerted on the way out), but the Return button reads off track through the pause. That is the lag cost the coder named, measured; the S22 check should expect it until -10 lands.

### 1.4 402a2-1 (Medium, for the owner): the place half latches

`placeOnWayHome` never moves the place back while a node from the previous place on is within 50 m (`WayHomeProgress.kt:132-135`, `from = previous.index`; this is the rule -1613 chose for the needle, so a switchback's later leg is never taken). Half (a) reads `alongMeters[place.index] > 0`, so once the place has left node 0 it stays left until a new line is published (`WayHomeWatch.kt:197-198`), and the dotted part then counts at every fix short of the start, whatever the walker does there.

Two ways the place leaves node 0:

- **By walking back more than 25 m** (the ruled case). The coder discloses the consequence: "walking back out toward the start counts until the walker passes the start". What is not said is the standing form: a walker who came 30 m back along the dotted line and then walks out to where it starts and **stands there** is counted on every reading whose jitter lands ahead of the start and off on every reading behind it; the 15 s hold needs 15 consecutive off readings, so with ordinary jitter it never completes. The alert comes only when they walk on past the start by more than the jitter. The owner's words were "Only walking out along the stray (the place held at the line's start, or behind) raises the alert"; here the walker is at the start and the place is not, because the place does not come back.
- **By one admitted fix that jumps 26 m or more back along the line** (replica section 6: a 30 m jump moves the place to node 1, 7 m along; 26 m does not). After that, a walker standing at the far end with 3 m jitter gets no alert in 60 s (0 of 1, against 1 of 1 without the jump). The S22 reports a constant 3.79 m accuracy on every GPS fix (`LocationSampler.kt:23-27`), so the way-home gate (`WayHomeWatch.kt:129`, 50 m) and the judge's limit (`OffTrackJudge.kt:93`) both treat such a jump as exact. The misses at sigma 8 and up in 1.2 are this mechanism (P(a −25 m jump along the line) per reading is about 0.1% at sigma 8 and 0.6% at sigma 10, which over 60 readings is the 4% and 25% missed).

When it bites: only a walker not yet alerted (within about 6 s of the re-route) or already re-armed (10 s on course while heading back). The first alert of a steady stray is untouched. The coder's "a jump counts by (a) only while the walker stays short of the start" is true and is this case stated from the other side; what it leaves out is that "short of the start" includes standing at it.

Not a hold: it is narrow, half-disclosed, and the fix is a design choice the owner should make (for example, (a) only while the fix itself is more than 10 m ahead of the start, i.e. (a) AND `metersAheadOfLineStart > 0`; or the place half dropped and (b) alone, which the four entry-point tests already pass, section 4). If the owner wants it, it is one line and a test; I would not reopen this PR for it.

### 1.5 The lag of half (a)

The service gives each fix to the return watch before the way-home watch (`TrackRecordingService.kt:223`, `:239`, the same coroutine, no launch between them), so the place read at `:223` is the last reading's, as the coder says. Half (b) reads this reading. On the first reading after a re-route the place is null (`WayHomeWatch.kt:197`), so (b) alone decides, which is right: the walker is at or past the new start. A reading the way-home gate refuses (accuracy over 50 m, `:129`) leaves the place where it was; the judge's limit is then over 90 m (`OffTrackJudge.kt:93`), so that reading is on course by the path or trail almost anywhere the dotted part could matter. A network fix is not judged by either watch (`OffTrackJudge.kt:85`, `WayHomeWatch.kt:128`). A jumping fix: see 1.4.
