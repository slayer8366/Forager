## 0. Mechanism prediction (written before reading the diff)

Expected: a large release (about 1000 commits since main) carrying the navigation feature's finishing pieces (card kind line, needle, next-turn arrow, evening line, route card, Re-center, locate ring and glide), two database steps (22->23->24), a privacy-policy text change, CI memory. Where it would fail if it fails:

1. Records: two migrations in one Play update. A Play build has no destructive fallback, so a missing or wrong step is a crash at open. Check both migrations exist, are registered, and are tested (22->23, 23->24, ideally 22->24 in one hop).
2. Wrong direction, distance or time: needle, next-turn arrow, route card. Only phone evidence counts.
3. States that read as broken: a locate ring that stays, a bubble left open after its pin leaves the screen, a Re-center shown while centred, a tuck that looks like a dismissal.
4. Accessibility: arrow or needle with no spoken label; pulse with no Reduce Motion path; the card at the largest font.
5. Map chrome: route card, bubble, card lift at 80%; a Surface intercepting touches across its full bounds.
6. Back: route card, bubble and card each retrace one step.
7. Privacy: #510's text matches the build; no new network call from navigation.
8. Known bugs listed before users find them.

## Verdict: pass with items, on two conditions the gate already names

Nothing I found would crash the app, lose a record, send someone the wrong way, mislead about time, read as identification, break an owner ruling, or ship something unfinished. The owner ships or holds each item below. Two gate steps were still open when I wrote this and the verdict assumes they close green: CI on the exact commit (item 1) and the owner's real-phone upgrade check (item 2), which the philosophy requires for this promotion because storage changes (22 -> 24).

How the prediction fared: the migration chain is in order (prediction 1 held up well); direction (2) rests on one phone read of the turn rule, no field walk (item 6); broken-looking states (3) were mostly found and ruled by the S22 passes; accessibility (4) is better than predicted (the arrow speaks, the pulse honours Reduce Motion); map chrome (5) is at 80% and the strip's touch band is ruled; Back (6) matches the owner's order; privacy (7) is covered by #510 and the live page; the known-bugs list (8) is behind the record (item 4).
