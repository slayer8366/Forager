## B. What counts as a turn
- **B1, the planner's call (O1), provisional:** rule R(30°, 30°) from Phase 1, section 2.
  - A turn is only at a node where 3 or more ways meet.
  - It is a turn when another way there is straighter than the route's way, or when the route bends at least 30° and is the straightest by less than 30°.
  - Angles are measured 20 m either side, and every way in the data counts.
  - The thresholds were set on the public fixture. Phase 2 logs each junction's class, with no positions (M1). The planner reads that log on the S22 on the owner's own routes before the thresholds are kept, and any change comes back as a ruling.
- **B2, the owner (O3), to "Where the route first reaches a trail from where you stand, or leaves it for the last stretch to the waypoint, you have to pick left or right, but there's no junction in the map data. Show those as turns?":** **"Show them (Recommended)"**. The option read: "They get an arrow and distance like any junction. Picking which way along the trail is often the walk's first decision." The other: "Junctions only".
  - So the joins count too, by the same rule, with the trail's two directions as the ways there. This covers the way to a waypoint's lead-in and final leg, and the way home's two joints.
- **B3, the owner (O2), to "At a fork where your route carries straight on and another path splits off, should the card show anything?":** **"A keep-left/right arrow"**. The option read: "A fork arrow shows which branch to keep to, with its distance." The other: "Nothing (Recommended)".
  - **The planner's reading:** a fork is where the route keeps the straightest way while another way leaves within 45° of it (Phase 1's 70 cases). The arrow is keep-left when the branch leaves to the right, and keep-right when it leaves to the left. A fork is a "next turn" like any other: it shows until passed.
- **B4, the planner's call (O9):** each turn in its turn, never merged, dog-legs included.
