## What the owner wants

The owner, verbatim: "I want to do property ownership and private properties to guide people away from those areas." Asked what "guide away" means, the owner picked all of: show it on the map; warn on entering; routes avoid it; and let the research inform it.

Context: Forager is a free, open-source foraging app (no proprietary software, RECORD -397). It works offline, with downloaded map regions, and is building its own map service: weekly builds from OpenStreetMap on a home machine, served from Cloudflare (-879, -880). Trail navigation is planned on OpenStreetMap with a routing engine (docs/plans/2026-10-04-own-map-service-spec.md). It is US-first, Pacific Northwest first.

## Questions

1. **Data:** which sources say what land is public, protected, tribal or private, for Oregon and Washington first, then the US? For each: coverage, update rate, format, size, licence and the exact terms for redistribution in a free app and in offline downloads, and its known accuracy limits. Include the federal protected-areas database, state and county parcel data, and agency land datasets. Open each source page and cite it; a search snippet is not a source.
2. **Owner names:** what parcel-ownership data includes owner names, under what licence, and the privacy, legal and ethical concerns of showing named owners. Is showing "private, owner not shown" a recognised practice?
3. **Prior art:** what established outdoor, hunting and navigation apps do. Do they show public versus private land, owner names, warnings on entry, routing that avoids private land? What do they say about accuracy, and what disclaimers do they show? Describe the apps generically.
4. **Foraging rules by land type:** in Oregon and Washington, which land types allow personal mushroom picking, which need permits, which ban it (federal forest, BLM, national park, state forest, state park, tribal land), with sources. Could the layer say "picking allowed / permit / not allowed" honestly, or only the ownership?
5. **Guiding away:** for each of the owner's three behaviours, what the data can honestly support, given parcel accuracy and GPS error. A warning or route based on a wrong boundary is worse than none; how do others handle it? Can the routing engine planned (BRouter) take avoid-areas?
6. **Fit:** what a weekly build adds in size and processing for the PNW and for the US.
