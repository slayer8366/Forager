## The question this answers

Can Forager run the camera itself, through the platform camera interface, without losing anything today's CameraX camera does? And how big and how fragile is that? This is a prototype to measure, not the shipped engine.

## What to build (the prototype engine)

`PlatformCameraEngine : CameraCaptureSession`, in the debug source set. It does:
1. **Viewfinder.** It opens the logical back camera, with the main wide pinned by a physical stream where the phone supports it (-1079). It shows the preview in the dialog's existing viewfinder area with the same framing as today, and lives through lifecycle changes: background, foreground, screen off and on.
2. **Focus and exposure,** as today's camera does them. Find out what that is (continuous autofocus? tap to focus?) and match it.
3. **Flash:** off, on and auto, as `FlashMode` defines them, with the platform's precapture sequence for flash shots.
4. **Single JPG capture** through `capture()`. The output matches today's: same size class, the orientation tag right for the device rotation, and the scrub applied on the existing persist path.
5. **Single RAW capture** through `captureRaw()`. RAW plus JPG from one exposure, with noise reduction OFF on the RAW request (-1064), the active physical camera logged, and the DNG written by `ForagerDngWriter` exactly as stage 2 does.
6. **An 8-frame RAW burst,** a debug method only, no UI. It runs inside the running session, with no camera hand-over and no viewfinder blink. Exposure and white balance are taken from the live preview and locked. Frames are discarded; it logs the rate and the shutter-to-last-frame time.
7. **Recovery.** If the camera is disconnected (another app takes it), report it in `CameraSessionState` and recover when it's free again, as today's camera does.
8. **Fallback.** Any open or configure failure falls back to the CameraX engine for that session, logged with the reason.
