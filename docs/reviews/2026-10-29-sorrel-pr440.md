## 4. The steadying: the first minute, measured

The dispatch asks whether a walker would see the first minute's median anchor step the reading, and how big it is on the 10-28 walk.
I replayed `LiveHeight`'s rule as shipped (upper-middle median for the anchor, mean-of-two median for pressure, the anchor's time
left at the first fix through the window, as `LiveHeight.kt:92-97` has it) on the walk's stored points, causally, with the geoid as a
constant offset (it cancels out of every step). The script is in my scratchpad, not committed; the figures are heights, times and
counts only.

**10-28 walk, 84 stored points 5 to 7 s apart, flat.** The first fix shows the GPS height as it is (the anchor equals its own
difference). At 7 s the second point's GPS is 8.1 m lower, but the upper-middle median of two keeps the first difference, so the
shown height moves 0.1 m. At 13 s the third point makes the median the middle one, and the shown height drops **7.0 m** in one step
(about 23 ft). Two more steps over 1 m follow at 44 s and 54 s (1.1 and 1.2 m). After the first minute the largest step in the
remaining 72 is **0.8 m**, and none reaches 1 m. For comparison, raw GPS stepped 8.1 m in that minute.

**10-24 recording B, hilly, 678 points.** The first stored point carries no pressure, so the first two shown values are the converted
GPS as it comes (steps of 15.8 and 9.4 m in the first 16 s, GPS's own); once the barometer has a reading, the first-minute steps are
1.1 and 1.7 m. After the first minute the largest steps are 5.3 m, at gaps of over a minute between stored points (real movement, as
the report found). **Recording A** has no stored point inside its first minute, so it cannot show the effect.

**Would a walker see it?** Yes: on the 10-28 walk the strip would read about 23 ft too high for the first thirteen seconds and then
drop 23 ft at once, and tick by a few feet twice more before the minute is out. That is the price of showing a height from the first
fix while the anchor forms, and it is as large as the first few fixes' GPS error, not larger. After the minute the reading moves by
under a metre between points. **Two limits of this measurement:** live, fixes and readings arrive once a second, so by 13 s the median
holds about 13 differences rather than 3, and a single wrong fix moves it less; and the barometer's first batch may arrive up to 5 s
late (`PRESSURE_MAX_REPORT_LATENCY_MICROS`), so the first few shown values are converted GPS, stepping as GPS does. Neither can be
measured on stored points. The S22 step 3 ("steady while still", two minutes) is where the live size shows; it is an observation, not
a gate, and I'd keep it that way until the owner has seen it.

**The report's figure.** The altitude report's "4.1 m largest step, at the second point" came from Hazel's script, whose anchor
median is the mean of the two middle values; the shipped rule's is the upper-middle (the journal's), which on the same points gives
7.0 m at the third point. The report's figure described the script, not the code (CLAUDE.md, a derived figure carried across a
boundary). The size is the same kind, the GPS noise of the first few fixes.

## Findings

No S.

- **N1. The first-minute step is visible, and about as large as the early GPS error.** `LiveHeight.kt:93-97`; measured in section 4:
  7.0 m at 13 s on the 10-28 walk. Failure path: a walker who opens the map and reads the height in the first quarter minute sees it
  jump by about 23 ft, which "Don't make me think" counts as a state they might read as broken. It is the dispatch's own rule ("with
  its existing settings and nothing new tuned"), the completion report discloses it, and the alternatives each cost something: hold
  the first fix's own difference for the minute (no step, but the first fix's error stands for a minute), show nothing until the
  anchor forms (a minute of "Alt unavailable" on every open), or show converted GPS until then (steps as GPS does). An owner call,
  not a fix; I recommend reading step 3's observation first.
- **N2. The barometer is read on every foregrounded tab, not only with the map open.** `AvailabilityLiveLocation.kt:81-86` ties it to
  the live-fix collection, which `AvailabilityViewModel.kt:442-449` starts at `ON_START` on every tab by an earlier decision
  ("Deliberately not need-gating"). The owner's question was "whenever the map is open"; the answer built is "whenever the app is in
  the foreground". Failure path: the Journal or Settings tab left open reads the barometer with no reader of the height. The cost is
  one sensor at 1 Hz beside a GPS registration that already runs there; the battery step 7 measures this scope as built, so its
  figure is honest about what ships, and the owner should know what it covers. Narrowing it is the same "separate, larger change" the
  ViewModel's comment names for GPS.
- **N3. No test pins the anchor's slow pull.** `LiveHeight.kt:99-105`. Set `fraction` to 0 (anchor frozen after the first minute)
  and all 7 `LiveHeightTest` cases and all 5 screen cases still pass: the flat-walk case moves by less, the gap and pressure-fall cases
  send no fixes, the 30 m case skips its one fix. Failure path: a later edit freezes the anchor and nothing turns red; the reading
  then never follows weather (about 8 m per hPa, `BarometricHeight.kt:181-184`) over a long walk. A one-case addition: steady
  pressure, GPS stepped 10 m up for an hour of fixes, the shown height ends within a metre of the new level and not at the old one.
- **N4. The live median may see far fewer readings than the journal's.** `AndroidPressureProvider.kt:42-44` asks for 1 Hz with 5 s
  batching and `conflate()`s the flow; the recording service keeps only the latest reading, which is what conflation suits, but
  `LiveHeight`'s 60 s window counts readings (`LiveHeight.kt:74-77`). When the sensor delivers a batch, the main-thread collector may
  take one or two of each five. Failure path: a pocket squeeze or a car door's puff has fewer neighbours to outvote it in the window,
  so the live reading is less steady than the chart drawn later from the same walk (whose points carry a reading every 5 s but are
  smoothed with the step limit and mean pass the live rule leaves out). Whether the phones batch with the screen on is unverified;
  step 3 would show it as a range wider than the chart's.
- **N5. A barometer failure takes the position with it.** The barometer is a child of `liveFixJob` (`AvailabilityLiveLocation.kt:81`),
  so an exception from the pressure flow fails the parent and the fixes stop, and under `viewModelScope` an uncaught exception ends
  the process. `AndroidPressureProvider.readings` (`:27-44`) only looks up the sensor and registers a listener, neither of which
  throws in normal use, and a missing barometer is `null` then completion, which is handled. Low likelihood; noted because the
  coupling is new, and the height is the less important of the two.
- **N6. The converter's catch covers the two documented exceptions only.** `AndroidSeaLevelConverter.kt:51-56` catches `IOException`
  and `IllegalArgumentException`, the two the altitude report names from memory; the platform's documentation was not checked here.
  Failure path: any other exception from `addMslAltitudeToLocation` leaves the fix collector and ends the process on every fix. If a
  source confirms those are the only two, this closes; it is listed under "could not check".
- **N7. "Logged once" is once per process, not once per reason.** `AndroidSeaLevelConverter.kt:61-63`. Failure path: the model fails
  to load at the first fix (logged), then later the platform refuses a location for a different reason, silently. CLAUDE.md's
  "logged when it fires" is met for the first fallback only. A per-reason flag would keep the log quiet in the common case and still
  name a second cause.
- **N8. A drawn track with no GPS height is labelled as if sea level.** `SeaLevelLabels.kt:51` gives it a zero offset; its heights
  are the barometer's standard-atmosphere level (`BarometricHeight.kt:40`, anchor 0). Reachable only in a multi-track entry where
  another track carries the heights (`EntryReport.kt:157-160` gates a lone track as `TooFewHeights`). The same numbers were shown
  before this branch, so nothing regresses; but the labels now claim sea level, and `Unsupported` (no numbers) would be the honest
  read for that track. Tiny, owner's call.
- **N9. Two unmeasured costs.** The labels are blank for a frame while `produceState` converts (`EntryReportSummary.kt:134-137`);
  harmless. The export converts every point through the platform, each in its own `withContext(io)` hop
  (`GpxSeaLevel.kt:22-23`, `AndroidSeaLevelConverter.kt:47`): on a long track that is thousands of conversions before the share sheet
  opens. The geoid lookup is in memory once loaded, so this is probably fast; the S22 step 6 would show a delay if not.
- **N10. Two statements to carry forward with their scope.** The report's 4.1 m first-minute step is the script's median, not the
  shipped one (section 4). And the completion report's "the barometer is read whenever the map's live collection runs" is exact; the
  dispatch's and the owner's "whenever the map is open" is narrower (N2). Neither needs a code change; both belong beside the
  figures wherever they are quoted next.
