## 3. Ideas

**Idea 1: a context ceiling for main sessions as well as helpers.**
- *Pattern:* the largest session (d20e5247) grew past 200,000 tokens and carried on to 619,214. Across the day, 10 sessions passed 200,000. Each turn past that point re-reads the whole context.
- *Change suggested:* the planner's approved rule ("a fresh helper past about 200,000 tokens", in the RECORD entry for "#402 merges") covers helpers only. Ask whether the same handoff point applies to main sessions. This is a proposal for the owner, not a change.
- *Saving:* ceiling of 57,496,411 tokens (23.8% of the day), which is the input-side tokens above 200,000 in those turns (the script's upper bound). The real saving is lower: a fresh start costs its own handoff and re-reads, and the logs do not measure that cost. Stated as a range, between zero and the ceiling.
- *Risk to the result:* a handoff that drops a fact changes what the next session knows. The RECORD and the handoff note are the mitigation; the same risk the helper rule already carries.

**Idea 2: one background wait instead of short sleeps.** Ruled already ("one background wait, then stop", coder profile). New figures for the ruling:
- *Pattern:* Hazel issued 230 sleep commands on the day, asking for 1,797 seconds in total, an average of about 7.8 seconds each. These sat in 140 turns costing 21,618,028 tokens (9.0% of the day), about 154,000 tokens per polling turn, because each turn re-reads the context.
- *Change suggested:* the already-approved rule, applied: one background wait per check, then the turn ends.
- *Saving, inferred:* if roughly 20 background waits had replaced the 140 polling turns, about 120 turns at about 154,000 tokens would be saved, roughly 18.5 M (7.7%). The 20 is an assumption, not a logged figure.
- *Risk to the result:* a wait that returns late instead of being polled every few seconds. The check is the same; only the timing of the reaction changes.

No other idea had a figure behind it on this day. Helper prompts were small (medians 1,129 to 1,218 characters). Repeated file reads were negligible (Sorrel: 2 extra reads; Hazel: none).
