## 3. Ideas

Each idea gives the pattern, the change, what it saves (inferred where marked) and what it risks. Savings are upper bounds unless stated.

**Idea 1. One background wait, not polling.** *(The wording is ruled: -1645 approved "one background wait, then stop" for the coder profile. These are new figures for it.)*
- Pattern: 512 sleep commands on the day, 3,484 seconds asked (about 7 seconds each), issued in 362 turns that cost 80.8 million tokens (8.7%). Hazel issued 393 of the commands (about 3 seconds each, 56.0 million tokens across 248 turns); Rowan 73 (15.6 million); unassigned sessions 32 (6.7 million).
- Why it costs: each poll is a new turn that re-reads the whole context.
- Change: one background wait that exits on success, then the session stops.
- Saves: inferred, up to about 80 million (8.7%). Some of those turns did other work, so the real figure is lower.
- Risk: none to the result if the wait checks the same condition. A wait with the wrong exit can miss a finish.
- Timing: -1645 is timestamped 03:40 UTC on the 26th. The script does not split the day, so I cannot say how many polls fell after it.

**Idea 2. Fresh helper past about 200,000 tokens.** *(Ruled in -1645 for the planner profile. New figures only.)*
- Pattern: 1,892 turns ran with more than 200,000 tokens of context. Those turns cost 579.9 million tokens, 62.1% of the day. Rowan's helpers: 570 such turns (147.7 million); Hazel's: 471 (128.8 million); unassigned: 471 (211.4 million).
- Saves: the ceiling is 201.1 million tokens of context above 200,000 (21.5%). That is what a cap could avoid at most, not a forecast; a fresh start also costs the handoff.
- Risk: a fresh helper without the earlier state can redo checks. The saving holds only if the handoff carries the state.

**Idea 3. Find out what the largest session was, then apply the same cap to its role.**
- Pattern: session dd088739 had no role matched, ran 441 turns, spent 198.9 million tokens (21.3%), and reached 833,935 context. Its five most expensive turns are the day's five most expensive. The naming rule (-1645: name and role first) did not reach it.
- Change: first identify its role. Whether a main session should get the 200,000 handoff is the owner's or the planner's call; I did not check whether any profile sets a cap for main sessions.
- Saves: inferred, not computed per session. The ceiling for all unassigned sessions is 116.9 million above 200,000.
- Risk: this may be the owner's own session, which is not mine to cap.
