## The question

On a waypoint route the HUD's large figure reuses the way home's figure unchanged (`ui/availability/AvailabilityWaypointRoute.kt:135-145` calls `placeOnWayHome` and `wayHomeAhead`; `routeMeters` is `WayHomePlace.remainingMeters`). That figure is the line's metres from the earliest node within 60 m of the fix anywhere on the line, plus 25 m when that node is on the trail part (`domain/wayhome/WayHomeProgress.kt:148-152`; RECORD -1618, -1620, -1613). Hazel's S22 check read it 200 to 280 ft (61 to 85 m) above what was left, 412 ft at the end of the trail with 131 ft to go (`prompts/preserved/2026-10-26-hazel-413-s22-check-report.md`, step 2). On a waypoint's short final leg that margin is most of what is left.

## Method

1. **A replica of the figure in Python**, in this folder (`waypoint_figure.py`), built from `WayHomeProgress.kt` at `a00742ffb`: the line's 10 m fill (`WayHomeLine.init`, `:30-67`), the four-tier place search (`:137-143`), the hop band (`PathHome.kt:123-134`), the figure's node (`:148`), the trail allowance (`:149-151`), and `metersPastLineStart` (`:170-180`); haversine distance as `GeoDistance.metersBetween`; the test ground as `Ground.at` (`WayHomeFakes.kt:22-26`, base 45.02 N, 120.01 W). The HUD's arrival rule as `hasArrived` (`domain/NavigationReadout.kt:67-71`: straight distance within max(15 m, 2 × reported accuracy)) and "Approaching" within 100 m (`:88`).
2. **Validate the replica before using it** against the literals `WayHomeProgressTest.kt` pins at `a00742ffb` (CI green on PR #413's run 40310887423, read from Hazel's report): hairpin total 570; fix (0, −50) → 570; fix (195, 0) → 405; fix (−35, −50) → 605; fix (−49.9, −50) → 619.9 and (−50.1, −50) off the line; (0, −50 − b) → 570 + b for b in 5, 10, 20, 30, 45; (20, −50) → 590; (0, n) → 570 for n in −50, −45, −30, −10. All within the tests' 0.5 m. If any fails, the replica is wrong and nothing below is cited until it is fixed.
3. **Options are implemented as variants of that one function**, each a few lines, and every figure in the report comes from the script's output file (`waypoint_figure.out`), re-derivable by running it. Hand arithmetic is given beside the key figures so they can be checked without the script.
4. **Cases** (metres east, north on the test ground; the waypoint route's three parts are lead-in (STRAIGHT), trail (TRAIL), final leg (STRAIGHT), as `FindWayToWaypointUseCase.kt:81-89` builds them):
   - **W, the S22 walk's shape, approximated:** walker at (0, −40), trail (0, 0) east to (350, 0), waypoint at (350, 40). Lead-in 40 m, trail 350 m, final leg 40 m: 430 m. Hazel's report gives those three lengths and no geometry; the bends of the real trail are not modelled, so W is a straight-trail stand-in and is said to be one.
   - **F, final-leg lengths:** W with the final leg 10, 25, 40, 100 and 250 m long (250 m is the engine's reach, `WayToWaypoint.kt:63`), square to the trail and in line with it.
   - **Bn, a bend near the end:** trail (0, 0) → (200, 0) → (200, 30), final leg (200, 30) → (240, 30).
   - **U, a U-turn (Sorrel's parallel legs, as a waypoint route):** lead-in (−40, 0) → (0, 0); trail (0, 0) → (200, 0) → (200, 30) → (0, 30); final leg (0, 30) → (0, 70). Legs 30 m apart, as RECORD -1623's accepted case. Also legs 20 m apart (the test fixture's spacing).
   - **R, a re-route:** on W, the walker strays to (175, 75) (over 50 m, `WayToStray`), the new line starts there: lead-in (175, 75) → (175, 0), trail (175, 0) → (350, 0), final leg as before. The walker then walks the new lead-in south.
   - **Error sweeps:** for W, Bn and U, the walker truly at points every 5 m along the line, the fix displaced by every radius 0 to 50 m (step 2.5 m) at every bearing (step 10°), the gate's whole circle. For each option: count of short placements, the worst short, the worst long.
5. **Every claim in the report is marked** read (in the code or a document at the commit named), computed (by the script, from its output), or inferred.

## Options to compute

- **O1, today's rule** (the baseline): earliest node within 60 m anywhere, plus 25 m on the trail.
- **O2, today's rule without the 25 m on the waypoint route:** the reach alone. `WayHomeProgress.kt:202-209` says the allowance is "now a margin on top" since Amendments 3 and 4, and the never-short argument at `:213-223` does not use it.
- **O3, the straight line once on the final leg:** when the walker's place (the needle's, `:139-143`, "not before the last place") is on the final leg, the figure is the straight distance from the fix to the waypoint; otherwise today's rule. The final leg is a straight segment to the waypoint, so on it the straight distance is the true remaining distance.
- **O4, the reach from the fix's reported accuracy, at the arrival's own standard:** reach = max(15 m, 2 × reported accuracy) + the 10 m node step, never over 60 m; allowance dropped. This is the "candidate E" Sorrel named and the owner deferred for the way home (-1623: "to be revisited with walk data on reported accuracy"), applied to the waypoint only, where the word "Arrived" already decides from the same quantity on the same target (`hasArrived`). Computed at a reported accuracy of 4 m (what Hazel fed the S22; the S22's own best case outdoors is 3.79 m, `LiveFixGate.kt:68`), 10 m and 20 m.
- **Not re-proposed:** "follow your progress" (B of the rising-figure options), which the owner declined at -1623 after Sorrel showed it short by 295 m on the out-and-back under a 20 to 40 m error; a waypoint route has the same shape wherever the trail passes near itself (case U). The theorem in that write-up's §1 stands: no rule that trusts a fix only to the gate's 50 m can read tighter than O1 and stay never short, so every tighter option here trusts something more, and the report says what.

## Predictions

- **P2.** On W under O1: 60 to 85 m long on the trail; at the end of the trail, with 40 m left, about 125 m (60 + 40 + 25), which is Hazel's 412 ft within 10 ft; on the final leg 85 to 110 m long; the last figure before "Arrived" (straight 15 m, 4 m accuracy) between 85 and 110 m (280 to 360 ft). - **P4.** O3 reads the exact straight distance once the place is about 25 m along the final leg, so the last figure before "Arrived" is 15 m; in the sweeps it reads short where a fix displaced 25 to 50 m toward the final leg puts the place on it while the walker is on the trail, by up to about 50 m on W and more on Bn (the trail's last 30 m plus the error); on U the place rule's "not before" keeps the place on the trail for most displacements, but some placement near the leg's start reads short.
- **P5.** O4 at 4 m (reach 25 m): 15 to 25 m long on the trail and on the legs; last figure before "Arrived" 25 to 40 m; short only for displacements over 15 m, by up to the displacement minus 15 m (so up to about 35 m at the gate's edge), and the count of short placements grows with the trail's own bends (Bn) and nearness to itself (U). At 20 m (reach 50 m) it is within 10 m of O2.
- **P6.** Bn adds about 25 m to O1's margin on the final leg (about 110 m at the leg's start) because nodes before the bend are within 60 m of the leg. U reproduces -1623's climb on the return leg under O1 and O2 (long by up to about 200 m 30 m from leg 1), and O4 at 4 m does not climb.
- **P7.** R: under O1 and O2 the figure holds at the new line's total for the first 60 m of the new lead-in, then (O1) rises by 25 m when the figure's node steps onto the trail (Hazel's second sample: 764 ft held for about 55 m, then 808 ft); under O4 at 4 m it holds for 25 m and does not rise.
- **P8 (the recommendation I expect to make, stated so a reversal is visible):** O2 is the safe small win and changes nothing the owner has ruled on for the way home; O4 is the one that makes the arrival read sensibly and has a precedent on the same screen, at the cost of trusting the accuracy field the code's own comments say not to build on.
