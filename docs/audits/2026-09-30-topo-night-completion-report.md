## Current build: Street under topo, crossfading over map zoom 9.5 to 9.7, pure V1 on both layers
Base `4194a6d8`; branch `topo-night`. The owner, after the hard switch: "Add the cross fade to the topo switch. I honestly considered that myself". And on why a switch at all, recorded in `BasemapStyles.kt`'s KDoc: "But it does look a lot better than switching to day mode suddenly. We have two tradeoffs and only one can break confidence in the app." Read as: a visible change of style that stays night is a tradeoff; a map that turns light below a zoom reads as night mode failing, which is the one that breaks confidence. That is why the Street switch plus the crossfade was chosen over a topo zoom limit, deeper tiles, the hillshade and the rejected dimming (each alternative and its reason is listed in the KDoc on `nightLowZoomBasemap`).

**What it does.** `styleJsonFor(OPEN_TOPO_MAP, night = true)`: the Street layer (`basemap-street`) has `maxzoom` 9.7 and the default opacity 1 (its paint is exactly today's three V1 properties); the topo layer (`basemap`) has `minzoom` 9.5 and a paint of the same three V1 properties **plus** `"raster-opacity": ["interpolate", ["linear"], ["zoom"], 9.5, 0, 9.7, 1]`. So below 9.5 only Street; from 9.5 to 9.7 both, topo fading in over Street; from 9.7 only topo (a layer's `maxzoom` is exclusive, `minzoom` inclusive, style spec at the tag). Everything else is as in the previous section: topo day, Street, Satellite and the offline style are byte-identical, and the caption credits both sources at topo night at every zoom.

**Evidence from the pinned 13.5.0 (read, not run).** (1) `raster-opacity` accepts a zoom interpolate: the style spec at tag `android-v13.5.0` (`scripts/style-spec-reference/v8.json`) gives it `"expression": {"interpolated": true, "parameters": ["zoom"]}`, `"transition": true`, range 0 to 1; the SDK's `PropertyFactory.rasterOpacity(Expression)` exists (`javap` on the 13.5.0 AAR's `classes.jar`). (2) Opacity applies **after** the brightness mix: `shaders/raster.fragment.glsl` line 33 `color.a *= u_opacity;` and line 53 `fragColor = vec4(mix(u_high_vec, u_low_vec, rgb) * color.a, color.a);` (spin, saturation, contrast and the brightness mix produce the colour first; opacity then scales it and its alpha); `raster_layer_tweaker.cpp` passes `.opacity = evaluated.get<RasterOpacity>()`. So V1 is untouched on each layer and, unlike interpolating `raster-brightness-min`/`-max`, a fade cannot pass through a flat grey. The compositing onto the layer beneath is assumed to be premultiplied-alpha blending (`out = c x a + beneath x (1 - a)`); the blend mode was not read.

**Why 9.5 to 9.7 and not the planner's 9.4 to 9.6 (measured).** OpenTopoMap's tile level is 10 below 9.5 and 11 from it (`round(mapZoom + 1)`); levels 7 to 10 are the tinted ones. A fade starting at 9.4 would fade level 10 back in. **How the test pins "no fading layer shows tile level 10".** `the fading topo layer is never drawn on tile level 10 or shallower` parses the night-topo JSON, takes the topo layer's `minzoom`, and asserts `rasterTileZoomFor(minzoom) >= 11` (9.5 gives 11, 9.499 gives 10); it then steps through every zoom where the topo layer and Street overlap, at 0.01 intervals, asserting the tile level is 11 or more at each. A `minzoom` of 9.4 fails with `topo minzoom 9.4 is on tile level 10; a fading topo layer must not show level 10 (the tinted regime)` (revert check 2 below).

**What the tile load across the fade costs.** From the tile-level rule (`round(zoom + 1)`, 256 px tiles, both sources): across 9.5 to 9.7 both sources fetch tile level 11, one tile per 194-256 dp (194 dp at 9.6), so a 412 x 892 dp view covers about 17-18 tiles per source: about **35 together against about 18 for one**, for 0.2 of a zoom level, in both directions of a pinch. Above 9.7 it is topo only; below 9.5 Street only. Street's extra tiles come from OSM's server; the topo tiles are the ones the hard switch already loaded from 9.5. **Not measured or read:** MapLibre's look-ahead and parent/child loading around the band; whether a source with no currently drawn layer stops fetching (if it does not, both load at every zoom, a cost nobody has measured); real bytes (sample tiles are roughly 20-50 KB each, so about 1 MB for one full view of both). OSM's policy permits "only the tiles needed for the current viewport"; during the fade both are needed.

**Choosing the treatment and the change-over.**
- *Half-amplitude V1.* Scaling V1's output (`min` 0.5) puts the mean at 0.208-0.233 and the ground at 0.196-0.239 on tile zoom 7-10: inside both bands. The scale was picked to centre both at once; matching the ground alone would want about 0.4 (mean 0.17, below the band), matching the mean alone about 0.6 (ground 0.26+, above it). 0.5 is a judgement inside that range, not a fit.
- *A step at 9.5, not a ramp.* The content jump is itself at 9.5 (tile level 10 to 11), so the paint changes where the tiles do. A ramp ending at 9.5 would bring the last half zoom of the old tiles back to V1, which is the light state being fixed; a ramp running past 9.5 would darken tiles that already look right, which the dispatch said not to touch.
- *How the flat-grey crossing is avoided.* The shader computes `min + (max - min) * rgb`; blending from an inverted paint (`min` > `max`) to a non-inverted one passes through `min = max`, where every pixel is the same grey. Both states here are inverted (`min` 0.5 and 1, `max` 0 throughout), so the slope `max - min` never reaches zero and no value in between, at any zoom, can do it. The same structure is why no ramp was needed to hide it.
- *Why no paint can make roads and labels both lighter.* The spin preserves each pixel's channel mean, and saturation/contrast/brightness are affine, so the output's channel mean is an affine function of the input's. At low zoom black labels (mean 0) < ground (0.4-0.6) < pale roads (about 0.76) in day, so an inversion puts labels above and roads below the ground, and no inversion the reverse. Proof by the tile data, not the SDK; it does not cover a perceptual-luminance argument across hues, which was not attempted.

## The model and its limits
`RasterShaderModel.kt` (beside `NightColour.kt`): `rasterShade` models `shaders/raster.fragment.glsl` and `raster_layer_tweaker.cpp` at 13.5.0 (read from the tag for this change: spin weights, saturation toward the un-spun pixel mean, contrast about 0.5, `mix(min, max, rgb)`, clamp, round to 8 bits). `rasterPaintAt` evaluates a paint object at a zoom, with numbers, `["step", ["zoom"], ...]` and `["interpolate", ["linear"], ["zoom"], ...]`; any other expression throws (no guessed value). `rasterTileZoomFor` is the tile-level rule. Check against today's V1: `rasterShade` with V1 equals `nightColorOf` within 1 level on every distinct colour of all 81 tiles (test). **Where it may differ on the device:** no bilinear resampling between tile pixels at fractional zoom; no parent/child tile cross-fade (`raster-fade-duration`, where two tile levels are drawn under one paint at once, including the moment the paint steps while the old level is still on screen); no premultiplied-alpha edges; no `raster-*-transition` easing of a paint change; GPU rounding. The model's V1 was measured against S22 captures at mean |error| 0.63/255 (swatch board, section 2); the zoom-dependent paint has **not** been measured on a device.
