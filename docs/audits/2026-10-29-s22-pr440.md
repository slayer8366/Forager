**The four candidates, from the code** (`LiveHeight.kt` at `6ce00d7cd`; `BARO_WINDOW_HALF_MILLIS = 60_000`,
`BarometricHeight.kt:175`):
- **The first-minute rule.** During each window's first 60 s, every fix resets the GPS-over-barometer offset, so the
  strip shows the fix's GPS height. A 1.8 m change sits well inside GPS's own scatter here: sea-level GPS has a 3.9 m
  sd against flat ground. A test held inside a first minute would show "no difference" by design.
  - Every window after 13:13:50 ran under 75 s, except 13:42:58 to 13:46:50.
  - The 13:14 screenshots are inside the first minute.
- **The 60 s median.** Once steadied, the strip is the barometer's height from the median of the last 60 s of
  readings, plus the anchor. A level held under about 30 s cannot move that median at all. A level held 1½ minutes
  moves it in full about 30 s in. The 30 s stop on the recording would not have shown.
- **The display's rounding is ruled out.** The strip shows whole feet ("Alt 448 ft"), so a 6 ft change is six units,
  not lost to rounding.
- **A fault cannot be excluded or shown.** Nothing records the strip's value, or the map's pressure, at the test.

**So:** if the test ran in any window after 13:13:50, the first minute, or the 60 s median with holds under 30 s,
would give "no difference" with no fault. If it ran in the 13:06:56 to 13:13:50 window, the only long one, no data
from that window survives. The data cannot tell which. The tools build (dispatch 2026-10-29-05) is the next step.
