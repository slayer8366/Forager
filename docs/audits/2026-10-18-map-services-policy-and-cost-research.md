# Map launch research: third-party tile, glyph and hosting terms for Forager at scale

Researcher: Juniper Sounding (Fable). Written for Rowan Waymark (planner); the owner decides.
Read date for every source: 2026-10-18 (UTC), unless a line says otherwise.
Nothing downloaded beyond the pages read; no accounts, nothing signed; nothing committed.

## Method (written before any source was read)

- One fetch per page, read-only, over HTTPS, with the fetch tool's default user agent. No bulk pulls, no tile requests made to any server to probe it.
- For each question: find the operator's own policy page (not a blog or forum summary), quote it verbatim with URL and read date, then give a reading for Forager marked **inferred, not legal advice**.
- Where a figure is quoted (price, limit), quote the number from the page as it appears; do not compute or round unless marked **computed**.
- Where the operator has no stated policy, say so; do not fill the gap with a plausible value.
- Each claim marked **read** (from the page), **computed** (shown arithmetic), or **inferred**.

Predictions, so a contradicting result is visible rather than absorbed:
1. OSMF's tile policy forbids heavy use by apps, bulk downloading and offline caching beyond a short term, and requires a valid identifying User-Agent; apps distributed widely are expected to use their own or a commercial tile server.
2. OpenTopoMap asks for attribution and has no formal capacity guarantee; likely asks heavy users to contact them.
3. USGS National Map services are public domain, free, but with no SLA and a request not to hammer; rate limits unstated or informal.
4. MapLibre demotiles is a demo server, explicitly not for production.
5. Protomaps daily builds are free to download under ODbL (data) with its own code/style under BSD/MIT-like licences; fonts under OFL.
6. OFL-licensed fonts (Noto, Open Sans, Roboto under Apache) can be served as PBF glyphs; the OFL permits redistribution with the licence attached.
7. Cloudflare Workers paid: $5/month base, 10 million requests included then $0.30/million; R2 $0.015/GB-month storage, Class A $4.50/million, Class B $0.36/million, egress free; Self-Serve Subscription Agreement has a clause about serving disproportionate non-HTML content — that clause was reported removed in 2023, to be checked.
8. Satellite: NAIP is public domain (USDA); Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX is CC BY-NC-SA, which may be a problem for a free app; hosting a global or CONUS image pyramid in R2 costs storage in the tens to hundreds of GB range for zoom up to ~12-13, more for higher zooms.

## Q1. OpenStreetMap Foundation tile usage policy

Source: https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/ — read 2026-10-18. Evidence: `evidence/osmf-tiles.txt`.

**Scope and stance.** "OpenStreetMap (OSM) data is free for everyone to use. Our tile servers are not: they are funded by donations and sponsorship, and capacity is limited." "We may block access, without notice, if your usage degrades the service. We may try to contact you if possible, but cannot guarantee this." "Availability is best-effort: there is no SLA or guarantee." "If you cannot meet these requirements, please use an alternative OSM-derived service (see below), or run your own."

**Apps at scale.** The policy has no numeric ceiling. Its only quantity words are "heavy or inappropriate use harms others' ability to edit and view the map" and §7: "Commercial services, or those that seek donations, should be especially aware that access may be withdrawn at any point: you may no longer be able to serve your paying customers if access is withdrawn." The recommendation list says: "Avoid hard-coding the tile URL; allow switching without needing a software update." and "Publish a contact email on your website or app store listing."

**User-Agent (§3.1, §3.4).** "User-Agent: Send a clear, unique User-Agent string that names your app and optionally includes a contact URL or email." Example given: `User-Agent: MyTownMaps/1.4 (+https://example.org; contact: maps@example.org)`. "Do not use a library default User-Agent, and never impersonate another app or a browser." "Many HTTP clients and Software Development Kits (SDK) use a generic User-Agent header (e.g. okhttp/x.y, Go-http-client/1.1, python-requests/x.y, Java/1.8, curl/x.y). Traffic that uses these defaults will be blocked because we cannot identify or contact the actual application." "Keep identification stable across requests and releases. ... Do not randomise or rotate the product name or contact details".

**Referer.** "Referer (web only): Browsers are expected to send a valid Referer header. Native apps usually do not have a referer, this is ok."

**Caching (§3.2).** "Never send Cache-Control: no-cache, Pragma: no-cache, or similar no-cache headers by default." "Honour server caching headers (Cache-Control, Expires, Etag). If your cache cannot read them, cache each tile for at least 7 days." "Use Conditional Requests using If-None-Match and If-Modified-Since when requesting tiles that have expired."

**Bulk and offline (§4).** "Bulk downloading is any pre-emptive fetching of tiles other than those a user is actively viewing. This includes, but isn't limited to: 'Pre-seeding' large areas or multiple zoom levels in advance. Building tile archives (e.g. .zip, .mbtiles) for later distribution. Automated scans across wide bounding boxes, especially at high zoom (z≥14). Headless bots that pan/zoom the map to force rendering." "Offline use is not permitted on tile.openstreetmap.org. Features such as 'Download city/country for offline use' or 'Save area for later' rely on prefetch/bulk downloading and are therefore prohibited." "Note: If you require offline maps, use self-hosted tiles or a provider that explicitly allows offline/prefetching. Vector tiles are often more suitable for this use-case."

**What happens on violation.** "Enforcement: Traffic using generic defaults, referer-stripping, or spoofed identities may be blocked without notice." "Enforcement: Prefetch/offline patterns place disproportionate load on community-funded servers and will be blocked without notice. Repeated violations may lead to longer-term or network-level blocks." §7: "Access may be blocked without prior notice."

**Alternatives named by the policy (§8).** "Community list: https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Raster_tile_providers", "https://switch2osm.org/providers/", "Run your own tiles ... http://switch2osm.org/", and vector tiles, which "where the provider terms permit can be packaged for offline use."

**Reading for Forager (inferred, not legal advice).** The app's live street layer is compliant in form: it uses the HTTPS URL, sends an identifying User-Agent with contact, and offline downloads come from the owner's own Worker rather than this server. The exposure is capacity, not form: the policy sets no number, states access can be withdrawn without notice, names donation-seeking services specifically, and tells apps not to hard-code the URL so they can switch without a release. The one code-level gap is the fallback at `ForagerApplication.kt:98-100`, where a failed client install sends MapLibre's default User-Agent — the exact traffic the policy says "will be blocked".

## Q2. OpenTopoMap usage terms

Source: https://opentopomap.org/about (German; the OSM wiki points here as the "Usage policy": https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/OpenTopoMap, read 2026-10-18). Read 2026-10-18. Evidence: `evidence/opentopomap-about.txt`. The page's encoding mangles umlauts in the saved text; quotes below restore them. Translations are mine.

**Licence.** "OpenTopoMap steht unter der Lizenz CC-BY-SA. Das bedeutet, dass die Karte kostenlos und frei verwendet werden darf, solange stets Namensnennung erfolgt und die Weitergabe unter gleichen Bedingungen möglich ist." (OpenTopoMap is under the CC-BY-SA licence. The map may be used free of charge and freely as long as attribution is always given and redistribution under the same terms remains possible.) Tile path: "https://{a|b|c}.tile.opentopomap.org/{z}/{x}/{y}.png". Required notice: "Kartendaten: © OpenStreetMap-Mitwirkende, SRTM | Kartendarstellung: © OpenTopoMap (CC-BY-SA)".

**Apps (FAQ).** Question: "Ich würde gerne die OpenTopoMap als Karte in mein Webprojekt oder meiner Applikation ('App') einbinden. Was muss ich beachten?" Answer: "Dies ist jederzeit gerne möglich, sofern unser Server z.B. durch Massendownloads nicht zu stark beansprucht wird. Außerdem kann keinerlei Ausfallsicherheit garantiert werden. Wir würden uns generell über eine kurze Information über die Verwendung freuen." (This is welcome at any time, provided our server is not overloaded, e.g. by mass downloads. Also, no reliability whatsoever can be guaranteed. We would generally appreciate a short note about the use.)

**Capacity and limits.** No numeric limit is stated anywhere on the page. Roadmap: "We updated our render server to a smaller vServer on 05.01.2026. If there are performance issues, you can select the backup server tiles." History: "Currently, OpenTopoMap has more than 2 million distinct users per month ... It was never my goal to make any profit, at the cost that I never gave any service or support guarantee. ... the connection to the University faded away and we now need to retire and return the machine." Also: "OpenTopoMap will be updated to vector tiles soon, which are technically more up-to-date and require significantly less computing resources on the server. However, vector maps require a completely different software stack at the clients."

**Contact.** "Wir bekommen täglich unzählige E-Mails. Weil OpenTopoMap ein reines Freizeitprojekt ist und wir alle berufstätig sind, können wir leider nicht jede E-Mail beantworten". (We get countless emails daily. Because OpenTopoMap is purely a spare-time project and we all have jobs, we unfortunately cannot answer every email.) Donations: "OpenTopoMap ist nicht als Verein oder ähnlichem organisiert und kann deshalb keine Spenden annehmen." (Not organised as an association, so it cannot accept donations.)

**Reading for Forager (inferred, not legal advice).** Permission to embed is explicit and the licence is free, but the operators say in their own words that the render server was downsized in January 2026, that they are retiring the university machine, and that nothing is guaranteed. They ask to be informed; they do not promise to reply. The app's attribution string at `Basemap.kt:122` drops "Mitwirkende/contributors" and the "Kartendaten/Kartendarstellung" labels from the notice the page says "sollte ... deutlich sichtbar sein" (should be clearly visible); whether a shortened form satisfies CC-BY-SA attribution is a legal question, not answered here. This is Forager's default basemap ("Topo (Recommended)", `Basemap.kt:52`), so of the three live third-party sources it is the one carrying the most of the app's traffic to the least-resourced server.

## Q6. Self-hosting glyphs

Sources, read 2026-10-18:
- SIL OFL 1.1 official text https://openfontlicense.org/open-font-license-official-text/ — `evidence/ofl.txt`
- https://github.com/openmaptiles/fonts README — `evidence/openmaptiles-fonts-readme.txt`
- https://github.com/googlefonts/opensans `OFL.txt` — `evidence/opensans-OFL.txt`
- Protomaps `fonts/OFL.txt` (above); maplibre/font-maker licence via API — `evidence/gh-fontmaker.json`

**OFL permission.** "Permission is hereby granted, free of charge, to any person obtaining a copy of the Font Software, to use, study, copy, merge, embed, modify, redistribute, and sell modified and unmodified copies of the Font Software, subject to the following conditions:" First condition: "Neither the Font Software nor any of its individual components, in Original or Modified Versions, may be sold by itself." (Remaining conditions — the licence travels with the font, Reserved Font Names, no promotion using the author's name, share-alike for modified versions — are in `evidence/ofl.txt`, lines 46 onward.)

**Which sets.**
- **Open Sans** (the stack Forager uses): `googlefonts/opensans/OFL.txt` — "Copyright 2020 The Open Sans Project Authors (https://github.com/googlefonts/opensans)" followed by the OFL 1.1 text. So the current Open Sans is OFL, not Apache (my prediction 6 was wrong on that detail). The demotiles PBFs were made from openmaptiles/fonts, whose README says "All fonts are either licensed under OFL or Apache." and lists "Noto Sans (patched by Klokan Technologies), Open Sans, PT Sans, Roboto, Metropolis". Its `LICENSE.md` URL returns 404 (`evidence/openmaptiles-fonts-license.txt`), so the per-font licence must be read from each font's upstream.
- **Noto Sans** Regular/Medium/Italic: OFL 1.1 per Protomaps' `fonts/OFL.txt`, ready-made PBFs in `protomaps/basemaps-assets` ("Use the Code > Download ZIP button").
- **Roboto, PT Sans, Metropolis**: named by openmaptiles/fonts as OFL or Apache; not read at their own sources here.
- **Tooling**: maplibre/font-maker is BSD-3-Clause (API `spdx_id`), and the Protomaps PBFs were "generated by font-maker".

**Reading for Forager (inferred, not legal advice).** Serving PBF glyph ranges from the owner's R2 is redistribution of an unmodified or modified copy, which OFL grants outright; the obligations are to ship the licence text alongside (an `OFL.txt` in the fonts prefix does it), not sell the fonts alone, and respect Reserved Font Names if a renamed derivative is made. Generating PBFs is arguably a "Modified Version"; the OFL permits that too under the same terms. A credit in the app's licences screen is the usual form. Note for Cloudflare: `.pbf` is **not** on the CDN's default-cached extension list (Q7), so a Cache Rule is needed or every glyph request hits R2.

The default extension table includes PNG, JPG, JPEG, WEBP, AVIF, GIF, SVG, TTF, OTF, WOFF, WOFF2 — **it does not include PBF or MVT**, and JSON (style files, TileJSON) is excluded by name. Cacheable file limit: "Free, Pro and Business customers have a limit of 512 MB."

**Reading for Forager (inferred, not legal advice).** The "disproportionate percentage of pictures" clause still exists but exempts traffic served through a paid Developer Platform product — which is exactly what Workers + R2 is — so tiles served by the owner's Worker from R2 sit inside the sanctioned route, and the Developer Platform terms say so in as many words ("can be used to host content"). The remaining exposure is the temporary-limit clause (undue burden), which has no number. Two operational points fall straight out of the quotes: (a) the offline endpoint is on `workers.dev`, which Cloudflare may rename with one week's notice; a custom domain removes that right; (b) a Worker in front of R2 pays $0.30 per million requests after 10 million, and each cache miss is one Class B read at $0.36 per million — with CDN caching on (custom domain, Cache Rule for `.pbf`/`.json`, long Edge TTL for immutable tiles) R2 reads collapse toward the tile count rather than the request count, while Worker invocations are still billed on every request. A public R2 bucket on a custom domain with Cache Everything, bypassing the Worker, is the cheaper shape for plain `{z}/{x}/{y}` tiles and glyphs; a Worker is needed only where logic is (PMTiles range translation, style assembly).

**Computed example (not a forecast).** At 1,000,000 monthly users × 300 tile requests = 300 M requests: Workers (300−10)×$0.30 = $87.00; CPU at the docs' 7 ms example ≈ (2,100 M − 30 M)/1 M × $0.02 = $41.40; R2 Class B on, say, a 10 % miss rate = 30 M × $0.36/M = $10.80; egress $0. About $144/month at that size; tenfold less traffic is about $14. Every number here is an assumption times a quoted rate.
