## Q3. Altitude

**Where the strip's altitude comes from: the GPS fix's raw altitude, with nothing done to it.**
- The strip shows `uiState.liveAltitudeMeters` (`ui/availability/CompactMapTopStrip.kt:100`), which is `liveFix?.altitude` (`ui/availability/AvailabilityUiState.kt:415`). That is set from `Location.getAltitude()` when `hasAltitude()` (`location/AndroidLocationTracker.kt:130`).
- Android documents that value as height above the WGS84 ellipsoid, not above sea level. This is from my knowledge of the platform; I didn't read it in the source here.
- The build has no sea-level conversion: no `AltitudeConverter`, `mslAltitude` or geoid code anywhere in `app/src/main` (a `git grep` at `86c99060` found none).
- The barometer is recorded on every point (`TrackRecordingService.kt:220`) and used for the climbing allowance (`domain/BarometricHeight.kt`, `ClimbAllowance.kt`), but not for the strip.
- No smoothing either: each fix's altitude is shown as it arrives.

**What the stored fixes say, 17:32 to 17:40:**

| Reading | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GPS altitude (ellipsoid) | −0.7 to 18.9 m | mean 7.9, spread 3.5; up to 8.1 m between fixes 5 s apart |
| Ground height under the walk, from the trail data's own elevation | 30.75 to 34.75 m | which elevation model the route build used is unverified, assumed to be above sea level |
| GPS minus ground | mean −23.8 m (−31.9 to −15.8) | |
| Barometer, height relative to the start | 5.7 m range | tracks the ground's 4 m (correlation 0.82); the GPS altitude barely does (0.23) |

**Is below sea level an artefact of the reference?** Yes, almost certainly.
- The offset of about 24 m between GPS altitude and the ground is the size of the geoid's separation from the ellipsoid in western Oregon, about 22 to 24 m, with sea level above the ellipsoid there. That figure is from memory: no geoid model is available offline on this laptop.
- **The strip shows ellipsoid height labelled "Alt" in feet, as if it were height above sea level.** So it reads about 75 to 80 ft low here. A walker on ground about 100 to 115 ft above sea level saw "19 ft" through "−22 ft".
- The jumps between those readings (about 40 ft within minutes, a few metres apart) are GPS vertical noise: about ±10 m, on top of the offset. The barometer over the same minutes moved about 19 ft in all.
- **The "−22 ft" reading itself isn't in the stored data.** The lowest stored fix is −0.7 m (−2 ft). The strip shows every live fix, while the recording keeps only some, and nothing was stored after 17:40:54.
- The same ellipsoid figure is what the Start and End markers store (18.9 m and 5.9 m). Wherever the app shows or exports a marker's or point's altitude, it carries the same 24 m offset. I didn't trace every reader.

**Options, for the owner to price (not chosen here):**
- (a) **Convert to sea level.** Use Android's own `AltitudeConverter` (API 34 and up) where it's available, and on older phones show it unconverted with an explicit "unsupported" path.
- (b) **Use a bundled geoid grid.** It works on every API level, at the cost of a data file.
- (c) **Barometer for changes, GPS for the absolute level.** Show height from the barometer, anchored to GPS converted to sea level. Steadier, but more parts.
- (d) **Hide altitude until it's sea-level corrected.**

The first two fix the offset. Only (c), or smoothing, would steady the jumps.
